1. Relative Atomic Mass

Understanding Isotopes & The Carbon-12 Standard

What is an Isotope?

If you look at the Periodic Table, you will notice that the atomic mass of most elements is not a neat whole number. For example, Chlorine has a mass of $35.45$. Why is this? You cannot have half a proton or a fraction of a neutron!

The answer lies in isotopes. Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons (atomic number) but a different number of neutrons. Because they have different numbers of neutrons, they have different mass numbers.

  • Protons: Define the element (e.g., all Chlorine atoms have 17 protons).
  • Neutrons: Define the isotope (e.g., Chlorine-35 has 18 neutrons, Chlorine-37 has 20 neutrons).
  • Electrons: Dictate the chemical properties. Since isotopes have the same number of electrons, their chemical reactions are virtually identical.

Relative Atomic Mass ($A_r$)

Because atoms are unfathomably small, weighing them in grams is impractical. Instead, chemists use a relative scale. The standard for this scale is the Carbon-12 isotope, which is assigned an exact mass of $12.00$ atomic mass units (amu).

The Relative Atomic Mass ($A_r$) of an element is the weighted average mass of all its naturally occurring isotopes, relative to one-twelfth ($1/12$) the mass of an atom of Carbon-12.

The Formula:

$$A_r = \frac{(I_1 \times \%_1) + (I_2 \times \%_2) + \dots}{100}$$

Where $I$ is the isotopic mass, and $\%$ is the percentage abundance of that isotope.

This data is typically gathered using a device called a mass spectrometer, which separates isotopes based on their mass-to-charge ratio.

Worked Solutions

Review these examples before attempting the practice questions. Click to expand each solution.

Example 1: Easy

Boron has two naturally occurring isotopes: Boron-10 (mass 10.01 amu) with an abundance of $19.9\%$, and Boron-11 (mass 11.01 amu) with an abundance of $80.1\%$. Calculate the relative atomic mass of Boron.

Step 1: Write down the formula.

$$A_r = \frac{(Mass_1 \times \%_1) + (Mass_2 \times \%_2)}{100}$$

Step 2: Substitute the values.

$$A_r = \frac{(10.01 \times 19.9) + (11.01 \times 80.1)}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{199.199 + 881.901}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{1081.1}{100} = 10.81$$

Note: The answer makes sense because 10.81 is much closer to 11 than 10, reflecting the 80.1% abundance of Boron-11.

Example 2: Medium

Magnesium has three stable isotopes: Mg-24 (mass $23.99$ amu, $79.0\%$), Mg-25 (mass $24.99$ amu, $10.0\%$), and Mg-26 (mass $25.98$ amu, $11.0\%$). Calculate the relative atomic mass of Magnesium.

Step 1: Extend the formula for three isotopes.

$$A_r = \frac{(M_1 \times \%_1) + (M_2 \times \%_2) + (M_3 \times \%_3)}{100}$$

Step 2: Substitute and solve.

$$A_r = \frac{(23.99 \times 79.0) + (24.99 \times 10.0) + (25.98 \times 11.0)}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{1895.21 + 249.9 + 285.78}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{2430.89}{100} = 24.31$$

Example 3: Hard (Algebraic Reversal)

Gallium has two isotopes: Ga-69 (mass $68.93$ amu) and Ga-71 (mass $70.92$ amu). The relative atomic mass of Gallium is $69.72$. Determine the percentage abundance of each isotope.

Step 1: Set up algebraic variables.

Let the abundance of Ga-69 be $x$.

Since the total abundance must be $100\%$, the abundance of Ga-71 must be $(100 - x)$.

Step 2: Substitute into the $A_r$ formula.

$$69.72 = \frac{(68.93 \times x) + (70.92 \times (100 - x))}{100}$$

Step 3: Solve for $x$.

Multiply both sides by 100:

$$6972 = 68.93x + 7092 - 70.92x$$

Group the $x$ terms:

$$6972 - 7092 = 68.93x - 70.92x$$

$$-120 = -1.99x$$

$$x = \frac{120}{1.99} = 60.3\%$$

Step 4: Find the other abundance.

Abundance of Ga-71 = $100 - 60.3 = 39.7\%$

Final Answer: Ga-69 is 60.3%, Ga-71 is 39.7%.

Example 4: Extension (Mass Spectrometry Ratios)

A mass spectrum for a diatomic element shows peaks at mass-to-charge ($m/z$) ratios of 158, 160, and 162 in a ratio of 1:2:1. Identify the element, its naturally occurring isotopes, and their relative abundances.

Step 1: Analyse the diatomic nature.

The peaks represent molecules ($X_2$). The masses are 158, 160, and 162. This means the atomic mass of a single atom $X$ is approximately half of these values: roughly 79 and 81.

Step 2: Identify the element.

Looking at the periodic table, the element with an atomic mass near 80 is Bromine ($A_r \approx 79.9$).

Step 3: Determine the isotopes.

The isotopes must be Br-79 and Br-81.

Step 4: Prove the abundances using binomial expansion.

If Br-79 and Br-81 are in a 50:50 ratio (or 1:1), the probability of forming the diatomic molecules is:

  • $Br^{79}-Br^{79}$ (mass 158): $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$ (1 part)
  • $Br^{79}-Br^{81}$ and $Br^{81}-Br^{79}$ (mass 160): $(0.5 \times 0.5) + (0.5 \times 0.5) = 0.50$ (2 parts)
  • $Br^{81}-Br^{81}$ (mass 162): $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$ (1 part)

This matches the 1:2:1 ratio perfectly, proving Bromine exists as $50\%$ Br-79 and $50\%$ Br-81 naturally.

Question 2

[2 Marks]

Silver exists as two stable isotopes: Ag-107 (mass 106.90 amu, abundance $51.84\%$) and Ag-109 (mass 108.90 amu, abundance $48.16\%$). Calculate the relative atomic mass of Silver to two decimal places.

Hint: Use the standard $A_r$ formula. Don't forget to divide the entire sum by 100.

Worked Solution:

$$A_r = \frac{(106.90 \times 51.84) + (108.90 \times 48.16)}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{5541.696 + 5244.624}{100}$$

$$A_r = \frac{10786.32}{100} = 107.86$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correct substitution of values into the relative atomic mass formula.
  • 2 Marks: Provides the correct final answer ($107.86$) rounded to two decimal places.

Question 3

[3 Marks]

Copper has a relative atomic mass of $63.55$. It consists of two isotopes: Cu-63 (mass $62.93$ amu) and Cu-65 (mass $64.93$ amu). Determine the percentage abundance of the Cu-63 isotope.

Hint: Let the abundance of Cu-63 be $x$. The abundance of Cu-65 will then be $(100 - x)$. Use algebra to solve for $x$.

Worked Solution:

Let abundance of Cu-63 = $x$

Let abundance of Cu-65 = $100 - x$

$$63.55 = \frac{(62.93 \times x) + (64.93 \times (100 - x))}{100}$$

$$6355 = 62.93x + 6493 - 64.93x$$

$$6355 - 6493 = -2.00x$$

$$-138 = -2.00x$$

$$x = 69\%$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly sets up the algebraic expression defining the two abundances as $x$ and $100-x$.
  • 2 Marks: Correct substitution into the $A_r$ formula and attempts algebraic expansion.
  • 3 Marks: Correctly calculates the final abundance of Cu-63 as $69\%$.

Question 4

[4 Marks]

A sample of Silicon is analysed in a mass spectrometer. The output yields the following data:

Isotope Isotopic Mass (amu) Relative Intensity
Si-28 27.98 100.0
Si-29 28.98 5.10
Si-30 29.97 3.40

Calculate the relative atomic mass of this Silicon sample. (Note carefully: The data provides relative intensity, not percentage abundance).

Hint: Relative intensity does not necessarily add up to 100. First, find the total intensity by adding the three values together. Then, calculate the percentage abundance for each isotope before using the $A_r$ formula (or divide the sum of (mass × intensity) by the total intensity).

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Calculate Total Intensity.

Total Intensity = $100.0 + 5.10 + 3.40 = 108.5$

Step 2: Modify the $A_r$ formula to divide by total intensity instead of 100.

$$A_r = \frac{(Mass_1 \times Int_1) + (Mass_2 \times Int_2) + (Mass_3 \times Int_3)}{\text{Total Intensity}}$$

$$A_r = \frac{(27.98 \times 100.0) + (28.98 \times 5.10) + (29.97 \times 3.40)}{108.5}$$

$$A_r = \frac{2798 + 147.798 + 101.898}{108.5}$$

$$A_r = \frac{3047.696}{108.5} = 28.09$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Recognises that relative intensity must be summed ($108.5$) rather than assuming it equals 100.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly substitutes mass and intensity values into the numerator.
  • 3 Marks: Divides the sum of the numerator by the calculated total intensity ($108.5$).
  • 4 Marks: Correct final answer of $28.09$.
