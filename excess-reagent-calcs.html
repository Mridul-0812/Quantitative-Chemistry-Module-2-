14. Excess Reagent Calculations

Determining the leftovers: How much is remaining?

The Concept of the "Leftover"

In any chemical reaction involving a **Limiting Reagent**, the other reactant(s) are present in **Excess**. Once the Limiting Reagent is entirely consumed, the reaction stops, leaving a specific amount of the excess reactant unreacted. In quantitative chemistry, we must be able to calculate exactly how many grams or moles are "left in the beaker".

The Protocol: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Identify the Limiting Reagent. Use the "Test Value" method ($n / \text{coefficient}$) for all reactants.

Step 2: Calculate Moles Consumed. Use the actual moles of the Limiting Reagent to find how many moles of the Excess Reagent were actually used up.

Step 3: Subtract from Initial. $$\text{Moles Remaining} = \text{Initial Moles} - \text{Moles Used}$$

Step 4: Final Conversion. Convert those remaining moles back into mass (grams) using $m = n \times MM$.

Interactivity 1: Visualising the Remainder

Consider the reaction: $2A + B \rightarrow \text{Product}$. Notice how $A$ is consumed twice as fast. Adjust the starting amounts and "React" to see what remains.

Interactivity 2: The Subtraction Logic

This tool demonstrates the mathematical relationship in Step 3. If you start with a certain amount, and the Limiting Reagent dictates a certain usage, the rest is "Excess".

Initial Excess Reagent (mol): 10.00
Amount Consumed (mol): 4.00
Remaining: 6.00 mol

Worked Solutions

Easy

Direct Mole Subtraction

A reaction vessel contains $5.0 \text{ mol}$ of $H_2$ and $1.0 \text{ mol}$ of $O_2$. If they react to form water ($2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$), how many moles of the excess reactant remain?

1. Find LR:
Test $H_2: 5.0 / 2 = 2.5$
Test $O_2: 1.0 / 1 = 1.0 \rightarrow$ **Oxygen is Limiting.**

2. Moles of $H_2$ used:
Using ratio $2:1$, $1.0 \text{ mol } O_2$ uses $2.0 \text{ mol } H_2$.

3. Remaining:
$5.0 \text{ (initial)} - 2.0 \text{ (used)} = \mathbf{3.0 \text{ mol } H_2 \text{ remaining.}}$

Medium

Mass-to-Mass Excess

$10.0 \text{ g}$ of Magnesium ribbon is reacted with $10.0 \text{ g}$ of Nitrogen gas to form Magnesium Nitride ($3Mg(s) + N_2(g) \rightarrow Mg_3N_2(s)$). Calculate the mass of the excess reagent remaining.

1. Moles: $n(Mg) = 10 / 24.31 = 0.411 \text{ mol}$. $n(N_2) = 10 / 28.02 = 0.357 \text{ mol}$.

2. LR: Test $Mg = 0.411/3 = 0.137$. Test $N_2 = 0.357/1 = 0.357$. **Mg is Limiting.**

3. $N_2$ Used: $n(N_2 \text{ used}) = 0.411 \times (1/3) = 0.137 \text{ mol}$.

4. $N_2$ Remaining: $0.357 - 0.137 = 0.220 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.220 \times 28.02 = \mathbf{6.16 \text{ g } N_2 \text{ left.}}$

Hard

Deducing Products: Precipitation

A solution containing $15.0 \text{ g}$ of Silver Nitrate is added to a solution containing $10.0 \text{ g}$ of Potassium Chromate. Calculate the mass of the reactant remaining in the solution. You must deduce the products and include states.

Equation: $2AgNO_3(aq) + K_2CrO_4(aq) \rightarrow Ag_2CrO_4(s) + 2KNO_3(aq)$

Moles: $n(AgNO_3) = 15.0 / 169.9 = 0.0883 \text{ mol}$. $n(K_2CrO_4) = 10.0 / 194.2 = 0.0515 \text{ mol}$.

LR Test: $AgNO_3 = 0.0883/2 = 0.0441$. $K_2CrO_4 = 0.0515/1 = 0.0515$. **$AgNO_3$ is Limiting.**

Used: $n(K_2CrO_4 \text{ used}) = 0.0883 \times (1/2) = 0.04415 \text{ mol}$.

Remaining: $0.0515 - 0.04415 = 0.00735 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.00735 \times 194.2 = \mathbf{1.43 \text{ g } K_2CrO_4 \text{ remaining.}}$

Extension

The "Acid-Carbonate" Leftover

$25.0 \text{ g}$ of Sodium Carbonate is reacted with $25.0 \text{ g}$ of Hydrochloric Acid. Calculate the mass of the excess reagent remaining. Hint: Check the stoichiometry carefully.

Equation: $Na_2CO_3(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow 2NaCl(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)$

Moles: $n(Na_2CO_3) = 25.0 / 105.99 = 0.2359 \text{ mol}$. $n(HCl) = 25.0 / 36.46 = 0.6857 \text{ mol}$.

LR Test: $Na_2CO_3 = 0.2359/1 = 0.2359$. $HCl = 0.6857/2 = 0.3428$. **Carbonate is Limiting.**

Remaining HCl: $n(\text{used}) = 0.2359 \times 2 = 0.4718 \text{ mol}$.
$n(\text{left}) = 0.6857 - 0.4718 = 0.2139 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.2139 \times 36.46 = \mathbf{7.80 \text{ g } HCl \text{ remaining.}}$

Practise Questions

Question 1: Basic Excess Moles

[2 Marks]

In the reaction $A + 2B \rightarrow \text{Prod}$, you start with $10 \text{ mol}$ of $A$ and $10 \text{ mol}$ of $B$. State which reactant is in excess and calculate how many moles remain.

Identify the Limiting Reagent first by dividing moles by the coefficient.

$B$ is limiting ($10/2 = 5$ vs $10/1 = 10$). $A$ is in excess.
$10 \text{ mol } B$ uses $5 \text{ mol } A$. Remaining $A = 10 - 5 = \mathbf{5 \text{ mol}}$.

  • 1 Mark: Correctly identifies $A$ as the excess reagent.
  • 1 Mark: Calculates $5 \text{ mol}$ remaining correctly.

Question 2: Mass Remaining

[3 Marks]

$5.00 \text{ g}$ of Hydrogen gas reacts with $50.0 \text{ g}$ of Oxygen gas. Calculate the mass of the excess reagent remaining after the formation of water is complete.

The equation is $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$. Find moles first!

$n(H_2) = 2.475 \text{ mol}$; $n(O_2) = 1.563 \text{ mol}$.
LR test: $H_2 = 1.237$; $O_2 = 1.563$. **$H_2$ is limiting.**
$n(O_2 \text{ used}) = 2.475 / 2 = 1.2375 \text{ mol}$.
$n(O_2 \text{ left}) = 1.563 - 1.2375 = 0.3255 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.3255 \times 32.00 = \mathbf{10.4 \text{ g } O_2}$.

  • 1 Mark: Correct moles and identification of $H_2$ as limiting.
  • 1 Mark: Correct calculation of moles of $O_2$ used and remaining.
  • 1 Mark: Final mass with correct units and significant figures.

Question 3: Synthesis from Scratch

[4 Marks]

$10.0 \text{ g}$ of Aluminium metal is reacted with $40.0 \text{ g}$ of Copper (II) Chloride solution. Deduce the reaction products, balance the equation with states, and find the mass of the excess reagent remaining.

This is a single displacement reaction. Aluminium is more reactive than Copper.

$2Al(s) + 3CuCl_2(aq) \rightarrow 2AlCl_3(aq) + 3Cu(s)$
$n(Al) = 0.3706 \text{ mol}$; $n(CuCl_2) = 0.2975 \text{ mol}$.
LR Test: $Al = 0.185$; $CuCl_2 = 0.099$. **$CuCl_2$ is limiting.**
$n(Al \text{ used}) = 0.2975 \times (2/3) = 0.1983 \text{ mol}$.
$n(Al \text{ left}) = 0.3706 - 0.1983 = 0.1723 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.1723 \times 26.98 = \mathbf{4.65 \text{ g } Al \text{ remaining.}}$

  • 1 Mark: Balanced equation with correct products and state symbols.
  • 1 Mark: Correct moles for both reactants.
  • 1 Mark: Correct identification of $CuCl_2$ as the LR.
  • 1 Mark: Final mass calculation.

Question 4: Acid + Carbonate Challenge

[5 Marks]

$50.0 \text{ g}$ of Calcium Carbonate is placed into a beaker containing $1.00 \text{ mol}$ of Nitric Acid. Calculate the mass of the reactant remaining. State symbols required.

Acid + Carbonate $\rightarrow$ Salt + Water + Carbon Dioxide. Nitric acid is $HNO_3$.

$CaCO_3(s) + 2HNO_3(aq) \rightarrow Ca(NO_3)_2(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)$
$n(CaCO_3) = 50 / 100.09 = 0.4996 \text{ mol}$. $n(HNO_3) = 1.00 \text{ mol}$ (Given).
LR Test: $CaCO_3 = 0.4996/1 = 0.4996$. $HNO_3 = 1.00/2 = 0.50$.
**$CaCO_3$ is Limiting (by a tiny margin!)**
$n(HNO_3 \text{ used}) = 0.4996 \times 2 = 0.9992 \text{ mol}$.
$n(HNO_3 \text{ left}) = 1.00 - 0.9992 = 0.0008 \text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.0008 \times 63.018 = \mathbf{0.05 \text{ g } HNO_3 \text{ remaining.}}$

  • 1 Mark: Balanced equation with correct formulas and states.
  • 1 Mark: Correct mole calculation for $CaCO_3$.
  • 1 Mark: Correct LR determination ($CaCO_3$).
  • 1 Mark: Calculation of excess moles.
  • 1 Mark: Correct final mass.
