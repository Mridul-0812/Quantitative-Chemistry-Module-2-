Question 1: Hydrocarbon Analysis [10 Marks]

A gaseous hydrocarbon $C_xH_y$ is burned in excess oxygen. When $2.50 \text{ L}$ of the hydrocarbon reacts, it produces $7.50 \text{ L}$ of Carbon Dioxide gas and $10.0 \text{ L}$ of water vapour (all volumes measured at the same temperature and pressure).

a) Using Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes, determine the values of $x$ and $y$ and provide the molecular formula. [4 Marks]

b) Write the complete balanced chemical equation for this combustion reaction, including states. [2 Marks]

c) Calculate the mass of the hydrocarbon sample if the reaction took place at $100 \text{ kPa}$ and $25^\circ C$. [4 Marks]

Hint: According to Avogadro's hypothesis, at constant T and P, the volume ratio of gases is equal to their mole ratio. Start by dividing all volumes by the volume of the hydrocarbon ($2.50 \text{ L}$) to find the coefficients of the balanced equation.

MarksCriteria
9-10Correct MF ($C_3H_8$), full equation with states, correct mass using $PV=nRT$ or $V_m$.
5-8Correct MF, but errors in the mass calculation or missing states in the equation.
1-4Identification of volume ratios only.

Worked Solution:

a) Finding $x$ and $y$:
Volume Ratio $C_xH_y : CO_2 : H_2O$
$2.50 : 7.50 : 10.0$
Divide by $2.50$:
$1 : 3 : 4$
From the conservation of Carbon: $1 \text{ molecule of } C_x \rightarrow 3 \text{ molecules of } CO_2$, therefore $x = 3$.
From the conservation of Hydrogen: $1 \text{ molecule of } C_y \rightarrow 4 \text{ molecules of } H_2O$ (which contains 8 atoms of H), therefore $y = 8$.
Molecular Formula: $C_3H_8$ (Propane)

b) Balanced Equation:
$C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \rightarrow 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O(g)$

c) Mass Calculation:
At $25^\circ C$ ($298.15 \text{ K}$) and $100 \text{ kPa}$ ($1 \text{ bar}$):
$V = 2.50 \text{ L}$
$n = \frac{PV}{RT} = \frac{100 \times 2.50}{8.314 \times 298.15} = 0.1008 \text{ mol}$
$MM(C_3H_8) = (3 \times 12.01) + (8 \times 1.008) = 44.09 \text{ g/mol}$
$m = n \times MM = 0.1008 \times 44.09 = 4.44 \text{ g}$ (to 3 sig figs).

Question 2: Non-Ideal Gas Behavior [8 Marks]

A $1.0 \text{ mol}$ sample of an unknown gas was measured for pressure at various volumes at a constant temperature of $300 \text{ K}$.

Temperature (K)
Volume (L)

a) Plot the following data for an Ideal Gas: $(50 \text{ K}, 4.15 \text{ L}), (150 \text{ K}, 12.47 \text{ L}), (300 \text{ K}, 24.94 \text{ L}), (500 \text{ K}, 41.57 \text{ L})$. Connect with a line of best fit. [3 Marks]

b) Calculate the gradient of this line and relate it to the Ideal Gas Law ($PV=nRT$) where $P = 100 \text{ kPa}$ and $n = 1.0 \text{ mol}$. [5 Marks]

Worked Solution:

a) Plotting: The graph should be a perfectly straight line passing through the origin $(0,0)$.

b) Gradient Logic:
The Ideal Gas Law is $PV = nRT$. Rearranging for Volume as the Y-axis: $V = (\frac{nR}{P})T$.
This matches the form $y = mx + c$, where the gradient $m = \frac{nR}{P}$.
Calculation:
$m = \frac{(1.0 \text{ mol}) \times (8.314 \text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1})}{100 \text{ kPa}} = 0.08314 \text{ L/K}$.
Verification from data: $Gradient = \frac{41.57 - 4.15}{500 - 50} = \frac{37.42}{450} = 0.08315 \text{ L/K}$.

Award yourself (Max 8):

Question 3: Industrial Fertiliser Synthesis [12 Marks]

Ammonium sulfate, a common fertiliser, is produced by reacting ammonia with sulfuric acid. A reactor is charged with $500 \text{ g}$ of ammonia gas and $1.20 \text{ kg}$ of sulfuric acid solution.

a) Write the balanced chemical equation with states. [2 Marks]

b) Identify the limiting reagent using a formal stoichiometric comparison (mole ratio). [4 Marks]

c) Calculate the theoretical yield of ammonium sulfate in grams. [3 Marks]

d) If $1.35 \text{ kg}$ of ammonium sulfate is actually recovered, calculate the percentage yield. [3 Marks]

Hint: Ammonia is $NH_3$ and Sulfuric Acid is $H_2SO_4$. The salt produced is $(NH_4)_2SO_4$. Be careful with the 2:1 mole ratio when checking for the limiting reagent!

Worked Solution:

a) Equation:
$2NH_3(g) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow (NH_4)_2SO_4(aq)$

b) Limiting Reagent:
$n(NH_3) = \frac{500}{17.031} = 29.36 \text{ mol}$
$n(H_2SO_4) = \frac{1200}{98.08} = 12.23 \text{ mol}$
Ratio check: $n(NH_3)$ required for all acid $= 12.23 \times 2 = 24.46 \text{ mol}$.
We have $29.36 \text{ mol}$ of $NH_3$, which is more than required.
Limiting Reagent: $H_2SO_4$

c) Theoretical Yield:
$n((NH_4)_2SO_4) = n(H_2SO_4) = 12.23 \text{ mol}$
$MM((NH_4)_2SO_4) = 132.14 \text{ g/mol}$
$Mass = 12.23 \times 132.14 = 1616 \text{ g} = 1.616 \text{ kg}$

d) % Yield:
$\% \text{ Yield} = \frac{1.35 \text{ kg}}{1.616 \text{ kg}} \times 100 = 83.5\%$

a) Calculate the mass and moles of water lost. [2 Marks]

b) Complete the following stoichiometric table to find $x$. [6 Marks]

SubstanceMass (g)Molar MassMoles ($n$)Ratio

c) State the full formula and the name of the hydrate. [2 Marks]

Worked Solution:

a) Water Lost:
$Mass(H_2O) = 5.00 - 4.26 = 0.74 \text{ g}$
$n(H_2O) = 0.74 / 18.016 = 0.04107 \text{ mol}$

Question 5: Back-Titration Concept [10 Marks]

A $0.500 \text{ g}$ sample of impure limestone (impure $CaCO_3$) is reacted with $50.0 \text{ mL}$ of $0.200 \text{ M}$ $HCl$ (excess). The unreacted $HCl$ then required $12.5 \text{ mL}$ of $0.100 \text{ M}$ $NaOH$ for neutralisation.

a) Write balanced equations for both reactions with states. [4 Marks]

b) Calculate the moles of $HCl$ that actually reacted with the $CaCO_3$. [3 Marks]

c) Determine the percentage purity of the limestone. [3 Marks]

Hint: Moles of $HCl_{initial} = Moles of HCl_{reacted with CaCO3} + Moles of HCl_{reacted with NaOH}$. Calculate the total initial moles and the moles neutralised by $NaOH$ first.

Worked Solution:

a) Equations:
1. $CaCO_3(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow CaCl_2(aq) + CO_2(g) + H_2O(l)$
2. $HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) \rightarrow NaCl(aq) + H_2O(l)$

b) Moles of $HCl$ Reacted:
$n(HCl_{initial}) = 0.050 \times 0.200 = 0.0100 \text{ mol}$
$n(NaOH) = 0.0125 \times 0.100 = 0.00125 \text{ mol}$
$n(HCl_{excess}) = n(NaOH) = 0.00125 \text{ mol}$ (from 1:1 ratio)
$n(HCl_{reacted}) = 0.0100 - 0.00125 = 0.00875 \text{ mol}$

c) % Purity:
$n(CaCO_3) = n(HCl_{reacted}) / 2 = 0.004375 \text{ mol}$ (from 1:2 ratio)
$Mass(CaCO_3) = 0.004375 \times 100.09 = 0.4379 \text{ g}$
$\% \text{ Purity} = (0.4379 / 0.500) \times 100 = \mathbf{87.6\%}$
