8. Empirical Formula

Reducing Molecules to their Simplest Whole-Number Ratio

The "Blueprint" of a Molecule

While the molecular formula tells you exactly how many atoms are in a molecule, the Empirical Formula tells you the simplest mathematical ratio of those atoms. For example, Hydrogen Peroxide is $H_2O_2$, but its empirical formula is simply $HO$. It’s the "lowest common denominator" of the chemical world.

To find the empirical formula, we follow a rigorous, step-by-step table method. This ensures you never lose a decimal point or miscalculate a molar ratio.

1
List Elements: Identify which elements are in the compound.
2
Assume 100g: If given percentages, treat them directly as mass in grams (e.g., 25% becomes 25g).
3
Find Moles ($n$): Divide the mass of each element by its relative atomic mass ($MM$) from the periodic table ($n = m/MM$).
4
Divide by Smallest: Divide all mole values by the smallest mole value calculated in Step 3.
5
Simplify to Whole Numbers: If you have decimals like .5 or .33, multiply ALL values by the same factor to get whole numbers.

The Master Table Layout

When solving empirical formula problems for NESA exams, layout is everything. Use this exact table structure to keep your working meticulous and clear. It makes it nearly impossible for a marker to take away marks for "lack of working".

Element Mass / % ($m$) Atomic Mass ($MM$) Moles ($n = m/MM$) Molar Ratio ($\div$ by smallest) Simplified Ratio
Element 1 Value From Table Result Decimal Whole Number
Element 2 Value From Table Result Decimal Whole Number

Handling the "Nasty Decimals"

Sometimes, "Divide by Smallest" leaves you with a decimal. NEVER round these off unless they are very close (e.g., 1.01 or 1.99). If they are substantial decimals, you must multiply the whole set:

.50 Multiply by 2
.33 / .66 Multiply by 3
.25 / .75 Multiply by 4
.20 / .40 / .60 / .80 Multiply by 5

Worked Solutions

Example 1: The Basics (Easy)

A compound contains $75.0\text{%}$ Carbon and $25.0\text{%}$ Hydrogen by mass. Determine its empirical formula.

Element Mass (g) $MM$ (g/mol) Moles ($n$) Ratio Simplified
C 75.0 12.01 6.244 $6.244 / 6.244 = 1$ 1
H 25.0 1.008 24.801 $24.801 / 6.244 = 3.97$ 4

Empirical Formula: $CH_4$ (Methane)

Example 2: The x.5 Rule (Medium)

Calculate the empirical formula of an oxide of aluminium that contains $52.9\text{%}$ aluminium and $47.1\text{%}$ oxygen.

Element Mass (g) $MM$ Moles Ratio Simplified ($\times 2$)
Al 52.9 26.98 1.961 $1.961/1.961 = 1$ 2
O 47.1 16.00 2.944 $2.944/1.961 = 1.50$ 3

Note: $1.5$ is too far to round. We multiply both by 2 to get whole numbers.

Empirical Formula: $Al_2O_3$

Example 3: Three Elements (Hard)

A white powder consists of $32.4\text{%}$ Sodium, $22.6\text{%}$ Sulphur, and $45.0\text{%}$ Oxygen. Find its empirical formula.

Element Mass (g) $MM$ Moles Ratio Simplified
Na 32.4 22.99 1.409 $1.409 / 0.7047 = 2.00$ 2
S 22.6 32.07 0.7047 $0.7047 / 0.7047 = 1.00$ 1
O 45.0 16.00 2.8125 $2.8125 / 0.7047 = 3.99$ 4

Empirical Formula: $Na_2SO_4$ (Sodium Sulphate)

Example 4: Combustion Analysis (Extension)

Burning $1.15\text{g}$ of an unknown hydrocarbon produces $2.20\text{g}$ of $CO_2$ and $1.35\text{g}$ of $H_2O$. Find the empirical formula.

Step 1: Find mass of Carbon from $CO_2$.

$n(CO_2) = 2.20 / 44.01 = 0.0500\text{ mol}$. Since there is 1 C in $CO_2$, $n(C) = 0.0500\text{ mol}$.

$m(C) = 0.0500 \times 12.01 = 0.6005\text{g}$.

Step 2: Find mass of Hydrogen from $H_2O$.

$n(H_2O) = 1.35 / 18.016 = 0.0749\text{ mol}$. Since there are 2 H in $H_2O$, $n(H) = 0.1498\text{ mol}$.

$m(H) = 0.1498 \times 1.008 = 0.1510\text{g}$.

Element Mass (g) Moles Ratio Simplified ($\times 3$)
C 0.6005 0.0500 $1.00$ 1
H 0.1510 0.1498 $2.99 \approx 3.00$ 3

Empirical Formula: $CH_3$

Practice Questions

Question 1: Easy

[2 Marks]

A compound contains $80\text{%}$ Carbon and $20\text{%}$ Hydrogen. Calculate the empirical formula.

C: $80/12.01 = 6.66\text{ mol}$. H: $20/1.008 = 19.84\text{ mol}$.

Ratio: C = 1, H = $19.84/6.66 = 2.98 \approx 3$.

Answer: $CH_3$

  • 1 Mark: Correct moles for both elements.
  • 1 Mark: Correct final formula $CH_3$.

Question 2: Medium

[3 Marks]

A substance is $43.6\text{%}$ phosphorus and $56.4\text{%}$ oxygen. Determine the empirical formula.

P: $43.6/30.97 = 1.408\text{ mol}$. O: $56.4/16.00 = 3.525\text{ mol}$.

Ratio: P = 1, O = $3.525/1.408 = 2.50$.

Multiply by 2 $\rightarrow$ $P_2O_5$.

  • 1 Mark: Correct moles for P and O.
  • 1 Mark: Correct molar ratio ($1:2.5$).
  • 1 Mark: Correct whole number adjustment to $P_2O_5$.

Question 3: Hard

[4 Marks]

Analysis of a drug shows it contains $40.9\text{%}$ Carbon, $4.58\text{%}$ Hydrogen, and $54.5\text{%}$ Oxygen. Calculate the empirical formula.

C: $40.9/12.01 = 3.405\text{ mol}$. H: $4.58/1.008 = 4.544\text{ mol}$. O: $54.5/16.00 = 3.406\text{ mol}$.

Ratio: C = 1, H = $4.544/3.405 = 1.33$, O = 1.

Multiply by 3 $\rightarrow$ $C_3H_4O_3$.

  • 1 Mark: Correct moles for all three.
  • 1 Mark: Identifies the 1.33 ratio for Hydrogen.
  • 1 Mark: Multiplies all elements by 3 correctly.
  • 1 Mark: Final formula $C_3H_4O_3$.

Question 4: Extension

[5 Marks]

A $10.00\text{g}$ sample of a hydrated salt, $MgSO_4 \cdot xH_2O$, is heated. After all water is driven off, the remaining anhydrous $MgSO_4$ weighs $4.88\text{g}$. Calculate the empirical formula of the hydrate (find $x$).

Mass of $MgSO_4 = 4.88\text{g}$. Mass of $H_2O = 10.00 - 4.88 = 5.12\text{g}$.

$MM(MgSO_4) = 24.31 + 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) = 120.38\text{ g/mol}$.

$n(MgSO_4) = 4.88 / 120.38 = 0.0405\text{ mol}$.

$n(H_2O) = 5.12 / 18.016 = 0.2842\text{ mol}$.

Ratio: $MgSO_4 = 1$, $H_2O = 0.2842 / 0.0405 = 7.01 \approx 7$.

Answer: $MgSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$ (Epsom Salts)

  • 1 Mark: Correctly finds mass of water lost ($5.12\text{g}$).
  • 1 Mark: Correct $MM$ for $MgSO_4$ ($120.38$).
  • 2 Marks: Correct mole values for both parts.
  • 1 Mark: Correct ratio of 7.
