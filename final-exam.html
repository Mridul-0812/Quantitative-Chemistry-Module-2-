Section I: Multiple Choice Questions (10 Marks)

Question 1

[1 Mark]

Which of the following contains the greatest total number of atoms?

Hint: Look at the subscript numbers in each chemical formula. How many atoms make up one single molecule of each substance?

Worked Solution:

One mole of any substance contains $N_A$ particles. Atoms per particle:

  • He = 1 atom/molecule
  • $O_2$ = 2 atoms/molecule
  • $H_2O$ = 3 atoms/molecule
  • $CH_4$ = 5 atoms/molecule

Correct Answer: D

Question 2

[1 Mark]

What is the molar mass of hydrated Copper(II) Sulfate, $CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$?

Hint: The dot does not mean multiply! You need to add the mass of $CuSO_4$ to the mass of five $H_2O$ molecules.

Worked Solution:

$MM(CuSO_4) = 159.62$ g/mol. $MM(5H_2O) = 90.08$ g/mol. Total = 249.70 g/mol.

Correct Answer: C

Question 3

[1 Mark]

A compound contains 40.0% Carbon, 6.7% Hydrogen, and 53.3% Oxygen by mass. What is its empirical formula?

Hint: Assume 100g sample. Convert mass to moles and find the simplest whole number ratio.

Worked Solution:

$n(C)=3.33, n(H)=6.65, n(O)=3.33$. Ratio 1:2:1.

Correct Answer: B

Question 4

[1 Mark]

For the reaction $N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)$, 2.0 moles of Nitrogen gas are reacted with 5.0 moles of Hydrogen gas. Which statement is correct?

Hint: Divide available moles by the stoichiometric coefficient to see which is smaller.

Worked Solution:

$H_2$ test: $5/3 = 1.67$. $N_2$ test: $2/1 = 2$. $H_2$ is limiting. $n(NH_3) = 5 \times (2/3) = 3.33$.

Correct Answer: B

Question 5

[1 Mark]

What is the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 11.7 g of Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$) in water to make a final volume of 500 mL?

Hint: $n = m/MM$ and $c = n/V$. Convert mL to L.

Worked Solution:

$n = 11.7 / 58.44 = 0.2$ mol. $c = 0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4$ M.

Correct Answer: C

Question 6

[1 Mark]

A student has 25.0 mL of a 2.0 M stock solution of $HCl$. They add water until the total volume is 250.0 mL. What is the new concentration of the acid?

Hint: Use $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$.

Worked Solution:

$2.0 \times 25 = C_2 \times 250 \rightarrow C_2 = 0.2$ M.

Correct Answer: C

Question 7

[1 Mark]

A weather balloon contains 50.0 L of helium at 100 kPa and $27^\circ C$. It rises to an altitude where the pressure is 50 kPa and the temperature is $-33^\circ C$. What is the new volume of the balloon?

Hint: Combined Gas Law. Use Kelvin.

Worked Solution:

$T_1=300K, T_2=240K$. $(100 \times 50)/300 = (50 \times V_2)/240 \rightarrow V_2 = 80$ L.

Correct Answer: B

Question 8

[1 Mark]

Consider the combustion of propane: $C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \rightarrow 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O(g)$. If 20 L of propane is reacted completely with excess oxygen at constant temperature and pressure, what is the total volume of the products formed?

Hint: Volume ratio is the same as the mole ratio for gases.

Worked Solution:

$CO_2 = 60$ L, $H_2O = 80$ L. Total = 140 L.

Correct Answer: C

Question 9

[1 Mark]

Which of the following gases has the highest density at Standard Laboratory Conditions (SLC: $25^\circ C$, 100 kPa)?

Hint: Density is proportional to Molar Mass for gases at the same conditions.

Worked Solution:

$CO_2$ has the highest MM (44.01 g/mol).

Correct Answer: D

Question 10

[1 Mark]

A 2.43 g sample of an unknown alkaline earth metal ($M$) is completely reacted with excess oxygen to form 4.03 g of its metal oxide ($MO$). What is the identity of metal $M$?

Hint: Mass of O = 1.60g. Moles of O = 0.1. Ratio is 1:1.

Worked Solution:

$MM = 2.43 / 0.1 = 24.3$ g/mol. This is Magnesium.

Correct Answer: B
