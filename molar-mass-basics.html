2. Molar Mass Basics

Translating the Periodic Table into Grams

What is Molar Mass ($MM$)?

In the previous lesson, we explored Relative Atomic Mass ($A_r$), which gives us the average mass of an atom in atomic mass units (amu). However, you cannot measure 'amu' on a laboratory scale. We need a way to translate these microscopic masses into macroscopic, measurable quantities.

Enter the Molar Mass (denoted as $MM$ or simply $M$). The molar mass is defined as the mass of exactly one mole of a substance. The beauty of the chemical system is that the numerical value of the relative atomic mass is exactly equal to the molar mass.

  • Relative Atomic Mass ($A_r$) of Carbon: $12.01 \text{ amu}$
  • Molar Mass ($MM$) of Carbon: $12.01 \text{ g/mol}$

The standard SI unit for molar mass is grams per mole ($\text{g/mol}$ or $\text{g mol}^{-1}$).

Using the Periodic Table

To find the molar mass of any element, you simply read the atomic weight given on your NESA Periodic Table. To find the molar mass of a compound, you sum the molar masses of all the constituent atoms present in the chemical formula.

The Golden Rule for Compounds:

1. Identify the quantity of each element in the formula using the subscripts.

2. Multiply the quantity of each element by its atomic mass from the periodic table.

3. Add them all together.

Beware of brackets! A subscript immediately outside a bracket multiplies everything inside the bracket.

Worked Solutions

Review these examples before attempting the practice questions. Click to expand each step-by-step solution.

Example 1: Easy (Diatomic Molecules)

Calculate the molar mass of Chlorine gas ($Cl_2$).

Data: Atomic weight of Cl = $35.45$

Step 1: Identify the elements and quantities.

The formula $Cl_2$ indicates there are exactly 2 atoms of Chlorine.

Step 2: Multiply by atomic mass.

$$MM(Cl_2) = 2 \times 35.45$$

$$MM(Cl_2) = 70.90 \text{ g/mol}$$

Note: Always remember that elements like Hydrogen, Nitrogen, Oxygen, and the Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I) exist naturally as diatomic molecules. If a question asks for the mass of "oxygen gas", it means $O_2$, not $O$.

Example 2: Medium (Simple Compounds)

Calculate the molar mass of Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$).

Data: C = $12.01$, O = $16.00$

Step 1: Break down the formula.

The molecule contains 1 Carbon atom and 2 Oxygen atoms.

Step 2: Sum the atomic weights.

$$MM(CO_2) = (1 \times 12.01) + (2 \times 16.00)$$

$$MM(CO_2) = 12.01 + 32.00$$

$$MM(CO_2) = 44.01 \text{ g/mol}$$

Example 3: Hard (Compounds with Brackets)

Calculate the molar mass of Aluminium Sulfate ($Al_2(SO_4)_3$).

Data: Al = $26.98$, S = $32.07$, O = $16.00$

Step 1: Distribute the subscript outside the brackets.

The subscript $3$ outside the bracket multiplies everything inside by 3.

  • Aluminium (Al): 2
  • Sulfur (S): $1 \times 3 = 3$
  • Oxygen (O): $4 \times 3 = 12$

Step 2: Sum the multiplied atomic weights.

$$MM(Al_2(SO_4)_3) = (2 \times 26.98) + (3 \times 32.07) + (12 \times 16.00)$$

$$MM(Al_2(SO_4)_3) = 53.96 + 96.21 + 192.00$$

$$MM(Al_2(SO_4)_3) = 342.17 \text{ g/mol}$$

Example 4: Extension (Hydrated Salts)

Calculate the molar mass of Copper(II) Sulfate Pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$).

Data: Cu = $63.55$, S = $32.07$, O = $16.00$, H = $1.008$

Step 1: Understand the dot ($\cdot$) notation.

In chemistry, the dot does not mean multiply. It indicates that 5 water molecules are crystallised within the ionic lattice for every 1 formula unit of $CuSO_4$. You must add the mass of 5 whole water molecules to the mass of the salt.

Step 2: Calculate the mass of the anhydrous salt ($CuSO_4$).

$$MM(CuSO_4) = 63.55 + 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) = 159.62 \text{ g/mol}$$

Step 3: Calculate the mass of 5 waters ($5H_2O$).

$$MM(H_2O) = (2 \times 1.008) + 16.00 = 18.016 \text{ g/mol}$$

$$Mass\ of\ 5H_2O = 5 \times 18.016 = 90.08 \text{ g/mol}$$

Step 4: Add them together.

$$Total\ MM = 159.62 + 90.08 = 249.70 \text{ g/mol}$$

Practice Questions

Attempt the questions below. Ensure you use the atomic weights provided on the NESA Periodic Table for accuracy. Once completed, reveal the criteria to self-assess.

Question 1

[1 Mark]

Define the term molar mass and state its standard SI unit.

Hint: Think about the relationship between one "mole" and measuring mass on a balance scale.

Model Answer:

Molar mass is the mass of exactly one mole of a specific substance. Its standard SI unit is grams per mole ($\text{g/mol}$ or $\text{g mol}^{-1}$).

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly defines the term and states the correct unit ($\text{g/mol}$).

Question 2

[2 Marks]

Calculate the molar mass of Sulfuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$).

(Atomic weights: H = $1.008$, S = $32.07$, O = $16.00$)

Hint: Ensure you are multiplying Hydrogen by 2, Sulfur by 1, and Oxygen by 4 before adding them all together.

Worked Solution:

$$MM(H_2SO_4) = (2 \times 1.008) + (1 \times 32.07) + (4 \times 16.00)$$

$$MM(H_2SO_4) = 2.016 + 32.07 + 64.00$$

$$MM(H_2SO_4) = 98.086 \text{ g/mol}$$

(Acceptable rounding: $98.09 \text{ g/mol}$)

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly identifies the number of atoms of each element and their respective atomic weights.
  • 2 Marks: Correct final calculation with the correct units ($\text{g/mol}$).

Question 3

[3 Marks]

Calculate the molar mass of Calcium Phosphate, $Ca_3(PO_4)_2$.

(Atomic weights: Ca = $40.08$, P = $30.97$, O = $16.00$)

Hint: The subscript '2' outside the brackets applies to both the Phosphorus and the Oxygen inside. You will have 2 Phosphorus atoms and 8 Oxygen atoms.

Worked Solution:

Number of atoms: Ca = 3, P = 2, O = 8

$$MM(Ca_3(PO_4)_2) = (3 \times 40.08) + (2 \times 30.97) + (8 \times 16.00)$$

$$MM(Ca_3(PO_4)_2) = 120.24 + 61.94 + 128.00$$

$$MM(Ca_3(PO_4)_2) = 310.18 \text{ g/mol}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates the mass contribution of the Calcium atoms ($120.24$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly distributes the subscript $2$ to calculate the mass of the phosphate groups ($189.94$).
  • 3 Marks: Arrives at the correct final molar mass ($310.18$) with units.

Question 4

[4 Marks]

An unknown element $X$ forms a stable oxide with the chemical formula $X_2O_3$. The total molar mass of this compound is experimentally determined to be $159.70 \text{ g/mol}$.

Using the atomic mass of Oxygen ($16.00$), calculate the atomic mass of element $X$, and use the Periodic Table to identify the element.

Hint: Set this up as an algebraic equation: $2X + 3(Oxygen) = 159.70$. Solve for $X$, then find that number on the periodic table.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Set up the algebraic equation based on the formula.

$$2(X) + 3(O) = 159.70$$

Step 2: Substitute the known mass of Oxygen.

$$2X + 3(16.00) = 159.70$$

$$2X + 48.00 = 159.70$$

Step 3: Solve for $X$.

$$2X = 159.70 - 48.00$$

$$2X = 111.70$$

$$X = 55.85 \text{ g/mol}$$

Step 4: Identify the element.

Looking at the periodic table, the element with an atomic mass closest to $55.85$ is Iron (Fe).

Final Answer: The atomic mass is $55.85$, and the element is Iron (Fe). The compound is Iron(III) Oxide ($Fe_2O_3$).

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly sets up an equation equating the sum of the atomic masses to $159.70$.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly substitutes the total mass of the 3 Oxygen atoms ($48.00$).
  • 3 Marks: Accurately calculates the atomic mass of element $X$ as $55.85$.
  • 4 Marks: Correctly identifies the unknown element as Iron (Fe).
