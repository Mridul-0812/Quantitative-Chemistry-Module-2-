17. Molarity Formula ($c = \frac{n}{V}$)

The Core Mathematical Language of Chemical Solutions

Understanding Molarity

In the previous lesson, we explored various units of concentration (g/L, ppm, percentages). However, as a chemist, you will almost exclusively use Molarity. Why? Because chemical equations (like $NaOH + HCl \rightarrow NaCl + H_2O$) are driven by the ratio of particles (moles), not by mass or percentages.

Molarity describes the number of moles of solute dissolved in exactly one Litre of solution. The unit is $mol/L$, $mol \cdot L^{-1}$, or simply $M$ (pronounced "molar").

The Molarity Equation

$$ c = \frac{n}{V} $$

  • $c$ = Concentration (mol/L)
  • $n$ = Moles (mol)
  • $V$ = Volume (L)

⚠️ The Volume Trap

In the laboratory, you will almost always measure solutions using glassware marked in millilitres (mL). You cannot use mL in the molarity formula! You must always divide your mL value by 1000 to convert it into Litres before calculating $c$ or $n$.

The Extended Formula (Mass to Molarity)

Often, you will be given the mass of a solid solute (in grams) and asked to find the molarity. Instead of doing this in two separate steps ($n = \frac{m}{MM}$ then $c = \frac{n}{V}$), you can substitute the mole formula into the concentration formula to create a highly efficient single equation:

$$ c = \frac{m}{MM \times V} $$

Where $m$ is mass in grams, $MM$ is molar mass, and $V$ is volume in Litres.

Ion Concentrations in Solution

Furthermore, when ionic compounds dissolve, they dissociate into ions. Dissolving $1.0 \text{ mol}$ of Magnesium Chloride ($MgCl_2$) in $1.0 \text{ L}$ of water creates a $1.0 \text{ M}$ solution of $MgCl_2$. However, because the formula dictates two chlorine atoms per molecule, the concentration of Chloride ions ($Cl^-$) is actually $2.0 \text{ M}$!

Use square brackets to denote concentration of a specific species: $[Cl^-] = 2.0 \text{ M}$.

Worked Solutions

Review the meticulous, step-by-step working out expected in high-level HSC examinations. Ensure you pay attention to the unit conversions and significant figures.

Example 1: Finding Concentration (Easy)

Calculate the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving $0.500 \text{ mol}$ of Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$) in water to make a final volume of $250 \text{ mL}$.

Step 1: Convert Volume to Litres
$V = \frac{250}{1000} = 0.250 \text{ L}$

Step 2: Apply the Formula
$$ c = \frac{n}{V} $$ $$ c = \frac{0.500}{0.250} = 2.00 \text{ mol/L} $$

Final Answer: The concentration is $\mathbf{2.00 \text{ M}}$ (to 3 sig figs).

Example 2: Finding Mass Required (Medium)

A laboratory technician needs to prepare $500 \text{ mL}$ of a $0.150 \text{ M}$ solution of Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$). Calculate the mass of solid $KMnO_4$ that must be weighed out.

Step 1: Find Required Moles
Rearrange $c = \frac{n}{V}$ to $n = c \times V$. Remember $V$ must be in Litres ($0.500 \text{ L}$).
$n = 0.150 \times 0.500 = 0.0750 \text{ mol}$

Step 2: Convert Moles to Mass
Determine Molar Mass ($MM$) of $KMnO_4$:
$MM = 39.10 + 54.94 + (4 \times 16.00) = 158.04 \text{ g/mol}$
$m = n \times MM = 0.0750 \times 158.04 = 11.853 \text{ g}$

Final Answer: Weigh out $\mathbf{11.9 \text{ g}}$ of $KMnO_4$ (to 3 sig figs).

Alternative Method: Use $c = \frac{m}{MM \times V}$ in one step!

Example 3: Hydrated Salts (Hard)

Calculate the volume of water (in mL) required to dissolve $15.0 \text{ g}$ of Copper(II) Sulfate Pentahydrate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$) to produce exactly a $0.200 \text{ M}$ solution of Copper Sulfate.

Step 1: Calculate Molar Mass including Hydration Water
When weighing out the solid, the mass includes the trapped water molecules.
$MM = 63.55 + 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) + 5 \times (2 \times 1.008 + 16.00)$
$MM = 159.62 + 5(18.016) = 249.7 \text{ g/mol}$

Step 2: Calculate Moles
$$ n = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{15.0}{249.7} = 0.06007 \text{ mol} $$

Step 3: Calculate Volume in Litres
Rearrange $c = \frac{n}{V}$ to $V = \frac{n}{c}$.
$$ V = \frac{0.06007}{0.200} = 0.30035 \text{ L} $$

Step 4: Convert to mL
$V = 0.30035 \times 1000 = 300.35 \text{ mL}$

Final Answer: Required volume is $\mathbf{300 \text{ mL}}$ (to 3 sig figs).

Example 4: Ion Concentration in Mixtures (Extension)

$100 \text{ mL}$ of $0.50 \text{ M}$ Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$) is mixed with $200 \text{ mL}$ of $0.30 \text{ M}$ Magnesium Chloride ($MgCl_2$). Assume volumes are additive. Calculate the final concentration of Chloride ions ($[Cl^-]$) in the resulting mixture.

Step 1: Total Volume
$V_{total} = 100 \text{ mL} + 200 \text{ mL} = 300 \text{ mL} = 0.300 \text{ L}$

Step 2: Moles of Chloride from $NaCl$
$n(NaCl) = c \times V = 0.50 \times 0.100 = 0.050 \text{ mol}$
Since $NaCl \rightarrow Na^+ + Cl^-$, $n(Cl^-) = 0.050 \text{ mol}$.

Step 3: Moles of Chloride from $MgCl_2$
$n(MgCl_2) = c \times V = 0.30 \times 0.200 = 0.060 \text{ mol}$
Since $MgCl_2 \rightarrow Mg^{2+} + 2Cl^-$, the moles of chloride are double!
$n(Cl^-) = 0.060 \times 2 = 0.120 \text{ mol}$.

Step 4: Total Moles and Final Concentration
$n_{total}(Cl^-) = 0.050 + 0.120 = 0.170 \text{ mol}$
$$ [Cl^-] = \frac{n_{total}}{V_{total}} = \frac{0.170}{0.300} = 0.5666... \text{ M} $$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{0.57 \text{ M}}$ (to 2 sig figs).

Question 1: Direct Calculation (Easy)

[2 Marks]

Calculate the number of moles of Hydrochloric acid ($HCl$) present in a $50.0 \text{ mL}$ beaker if the concentration is $2.50 \text{ M}$.

Hint: Use the rearranged formula $n = c \times V$. Don't forget to convert $50.0 \text{ mL}$ into Litres!

Worked Solution:

$V = \frac{50.0}{1000} = 0.0500 \text{ L}$

$n = c \times V$

$n = 2.50 \times 0.0500$

$n = \mathbf{0.125 \text{ mol}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly converts volume to Litres ($0.0500 \text{ L}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly applies the formula to find $0.125 \text{ mol}$.

Question 2: Mass to Molarity (Medium)

[3 Marks]

A chemist dissolves $8.00 \text{ g}$ of Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$) pellets in distilled water and transfers it to a volumetric flask, making the solution up to exactly $400 \text{ mL}$. Calculate the Molarity of the solution.

Hint: Step 1: Find Molar Mass of $NaOH$. Step 2: Find moles using $n = m/MM$. Step 3: Divide by volume in Litres.

Worked Solution:

$MM(NaOH) = 22.99 + 16.00 + 1.008 = 40.00 \text{ g/mol}$

$n = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{8.00}{40.00} = 0.200 \text{ mol}$

$V = 0.400 \text{ L}$

$c = \frac{n}{V} = \frac{0.200}{0.400} = \mathbf{0.500 \text{ M}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates Molar Mass of $NaOH$ ($\sim 40 \text{ g/mol}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates moles of $NaOH$ ($0.200 \text{ mol}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correct final molarity calculation ($0.500 \text{ M}$).

Question 3: Solution Preparation (Hard)

[3 Marks]

What mass of Barium Nitrate, $Ba(NO_3)_2$, is required to prepare $2.50 \text{ L}$ of a $0.150 \text{ M}$ solution?

Hint: Use $n = c \times V$ to find moles needed. Then calculate the complex molar mass of $Ba(NO_3)_2$ (don't forget the brackets mean 2 Nitrogens and 6 Oxygens!). Finally, $m = n \times MM$.

Worked Solution:

$n = c \times V = 0.150 \times 2.50 = 0.375 \text{ mol}$

$MM(Ba(NO_3)_2) = 137.3 + 2 \times (14.01 + 3 \times 16.00) = 261.32 \text{ g/mol}$

$m = n \times MM = 0.375 \times 261.32$

$m = \mathbf{98.0 \text{ g}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates required moles ($0.375 \text{ mol}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates Molar Mass ($261.32 \text{ g/mol}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correct final mass calculated ($98.0 \text{ g}$).

Question 4: Ion Concentration Mixture (Extension)

[4 Marks]

A chemist mixes $150 \text{ mL}$ of $0.20 \text{ M}$ Aluminium Sulfate, $Al_2(SO_4)_3$, with $350 \text{ mL}$ of $0.10 \text{ M}$ Sodium Sulfate, $Na_2SO_4$. Assuming the volumes are perfectly additive, calculate the final concentration of Sulfate ions ($[SO_4^{2-}]$) in the new mixture.

Hint: 1. Find total volume.
2. Find moles of Sulfate from Al₂(SO₄)₃ (Multiply moles of the compound by 3!).
3. Find moles of Sulfate from Na₂SO₄ (Multiply by 1).
4. Add moles together and divide by total volume.

Worked Solution:

1. Total Volume:
$V = 0.150 + 0.350 = 0.500 \text{ L}$

2. Sulfate from $Al_2(SO_4)_3$:
$n(compound) = 0.20 \times 0.150 = 0.030 \text{ mol}$
Because of the subscript 3, $n(SO_4^{2-}) = 0.030 \times 3 = 0.090 \text{ mol}$

3. Sulfate from $Na_2SO_4$:
$n(compound) = 0.10 \times 0.350 = 0.035 \text{ mol}$
Because subscript is 1, $n(SO_4^{2-}) = 0.035 \text{ mol}$

4. Total and Concentration:
$n_{total} = 0.090 + 0.035 = 0.125 \text{ mol}$
$[SO_4^{2-}] = \frac{0.125}{0.500} = \mathbf{0.25 \text{ M}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correct total volume ($0.500 \text{ L}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly identifies that Al₂(SO₄)₃ provides 3 moles of sulfate per mole of compound ($0.090 \text{ mol}$ total).
  • 3 Marks: Correctly finds total moles of sulfate ($0.125 \text{ mol}$).
  • 4 Marks: Correct final concentration ($0.25 \text{ M}$).
