Lesson 18: Dilutions

Applying the $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ Principle

The Concept of Dilution

In a chemical laboratory, it is highly impractical (and often dangerous) to store large volumes of dilute solutions. Instead, chemical suppliers ship highly concentrated stock solutions (e.g., $10.0 \text{ M}$ Hydrochloric Acid). When a chemist needs a weaker solution for an experiment, they perform a dilution.

Dilution is the process of decreasing the concentration of a solute in a solution by adding more solvent (usually distilled water). The critical concept to understand here is the conservation of matter:

When you add water to a solution, the volume increases, and the concentration decreases, but the total number of moles of solute remains exactly the same.

Think of it like adding more hot water to a cup of strong tea. The volume of the liquid in the cup increases, and the tea tastes weaker (less concentrated). However, the actual number of tea leaves (the solute particles) floating around in the cup hasn't changed at all!

The Mathematical Bridge: $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$

Because the number of moles ($n$) does not change during a dilution, we can establish a mathematical relationship. Recall from the previous lesson that $n = C \times V$.

The Derivation

Let state 1 be the initial concentrated stock solution, and state 2 be the final diluted solution.

  • Moles before dilution: $n_1 = C_1 \times V_1$
  • Moles after dilution: $n_2 = C_2 \times V_2$

Since the moles of solute are conserved ($n_1 = n_2$), we can equate the two expressions:

$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$

Where:

  • $C_1$ = Initial Concentration (Molarity of stock)
  • $V_1$ = Initial Volume (How much stock you extract to use)
  • $C_2$ = Final Concentration (Molarity of diluted solution)
  • $V_2$ = Final Volume (Total volume after water is added)

⚠️ The "Volume Added" Trap

The variable $V_2$ in the formula is the Total Final Volume of the solution. It is not the volume of water added. This is the most common mistake students make in HSC Chemistry exams.

If you need to calculate the volume of distilled water that must be added to achieve the dilution, you must use the following logic:

$$V_{\text{water added}} = V_2 - V_1$$

Worked Solutions

Study these exemplary worked solutions. They demonstrate the rigorous step-by-step mathematical communication expected by NESA markers in the HSC. Note the explicit stating of the formula, the substitution of values, and the final unit and significant figure checks.

Example 1: Finding the Final Concentration (Easy)

A student uses a volumetric pipette to extract $25.0 \text{ mL}$ of a $0.500 \text{ M}$ Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$) stock solution. This aliquot is placed into a $250.0 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask, and distilled water is added to the calibration mark. Calculate the final concentration of the diluted solution.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Extract the data
$C_1 = 0.500 \text{ M}$
$V_1 = 25.0 \text{ mL}$
$C_2 = \text{ ? (Unknown)}$
$V_2 = 250.0 \text{ mL}$

Note: Because we are using the $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ ratio, we do not need to convert millilitres to Litres, provided the volume units are identical on both sides of the equation.

Step 2: Apply the formula
$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$ $$0.500 \times 25.0 = C_2 \times 250.0$$ $$12.5 = 250.0 \times C_2$$

Step 3: Rearrange to solve for $C_2$
$$C_2 = \frac{12.5}{250.0}$$ $$C_2 = 0.0500 \text{ M}$$

Final Answer: The concentration of the diluted solution is $\mathbf{0.0500 \text{ M}}$ (to 3 sig figs).

Example 2: Finding the Required Stock Volume (Medium)

A laboratory technician needs to prepare $2.00 \text{ L}$ of a $0.150 \text{ M}$ Sulfuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$) solution for an undergraduate experiment. The only concentrated stock solution available in the chemical store is $18.0 \text{ M}$. Calculate the volume of concentrated stock solution required to prepare this dilution.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Extract the data
$C_1 = 18.0 \text{ M}$ (The concentrated stock)
$V_1 = \text{ ? (Unknown)}$
$C_2 = 0.150 \text{ M}$ (The target dilute solution)
$V_2 = 2.00 \text{ L}$

Step 2: Apply the formula and substitute
$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$ $$18.0 \times V_1 = 0.150 \times 2.00$$ $$18.0 \times V_1 = 0.300$$

Step 3: Rearrange to solve for $V_1$
$$V_1 = \frac{0.300}{18.0}$$ $$V_1 = 0.01666... \text{ L}$$

Step 4: Convert to practical laboratory units
$0.01666... \text{ L} \times 1000 = 16.66... \text{ mL}$

Final Answer: The technician must extract $\mathbf{16.7 \text{ mL}}$ of the $18.0 \text{ M}$ stock solution (to 3 sig figs) to make the dilution.

Example 3: The "Volume of Water Added" Trap (Hard)

A student has $50.0 \text{ mL}$ of a $1.20 \text{ M}$ solution of Potassium Permanganate ($KMnO_4$). The student wishes to dilute this solution down to a concentration of $0.0800 \text{ M}$. Calculate the exact volume of distilled water that the student must add to the existing solution to achieve this concentration.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Extract the data
$C_1 = 1.20 \text{ M}$
$V_1 = 50.0 \text{ mL}$
$C_2 = 0.0800 \text{ M}$
$V_2 = \text{ ? (This is the total final volume)}$

Step 2: Solve for $V_2$
$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$ $$1.20 \times 50.0 = 0.0800 \times V_2$$ $$60.0 = 0.0800 \times V_2$$ $$V_2 = \frac{60.0}{0.0800}$$ $$V_2 = 750 \text{ mL}$$

Step 3: Calculate the volume of water ADDED
The total final volume ($V_2$) required is $750 \text{ mL}$. However, the student already has $50.0 \text{ mL}$ of solution sitting in the beaker ($V_1$).
$$V_{\text{water added}} = V_2 - V_1$$ $$V_{\text{water added}} = 750 \text{ mL} - 50.0 \text{ mL}$$ $$V_{\text{water added}} = 700 \text{ mL}$$

Final Answer: The student must add exactly $\mathbf{700 \text{ mL}}$ of distilled water.

Example 4: Mass Dissolution followed by Dilution (Extension)

A chemist dissolves $5.844 \text{ g}$ of pure Sodium Chloride ($NaCl$) crystals in water and transfers it to a $100 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask, making it up to the mark. A $10.0 \text{ mL}$ aliquot of this solution is then transferred to a $250 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask, and distilled water is added to the mark. Calculate the final molarity of the $250 \text{ mL}$ solution.

Worked Solution:

This is a two-part problem. Part A: Find the concentration of the initial stock solution from the mass. Part B: Perform the dilution calculation.

Part A: Finding the Stock Concentration ($C_1$)
Moles of $NaCl$:
$MM(NaCl) = 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 \text{ g/mol}$
$n = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{5.844}{58.44} = 0.1000 \text{ mol}$

Molarity of Stock ($C_1$):
The stock volume is $100 \text{ mL} = 0.100 \text{ L}$.
$C_1 = \frac{n}{V} = \frac{0.1000}{0.100} = 1.000 \text{ M}$

Part B: The Dilution Calculation
Extract data for the dilution:
$C_1 = 1.000 \text{ M}$
$V_1 = 10.0 \text{ mL}$ (The volume of the aliquot extracted)
$V_2 = 250 \text{ mL}$ (The new volumetric flask)
$C_2 = \text{ ?}$

Apply $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$:
$$1.000 \times 10.0 = C_2 \times 250$$ $$10.0 = 250 \times C_2$$ $$C_2 = \frac{10.0}{250}$$ $$C_2 = 0.0400 \text{ M}$$

Final Answer: The final diluted concentration is $\mathbf{0.0400 \text{ M}}$.

Practise Questions

Test your understanding. Write down your mathematical working, then use the self-marking engine to evaluate your accuracy against strict HSC-style marking criteria.

Question 1: Standard Dilution (Easy)

[2 Marks]

Calculate the final concentration if $40.0 \text{ mL}$ of a $2.50 \text{ M}$ Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$) solution is diluted to a final total volume of $500.0 \text{ mL}$.

Hint: Identify your variables: $C_1 = 2.50$, $V_1 = 40.0$, $V_2 = 500.0$. Plug these directly into $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ and solve algebraically for $C_2$.

Worked Solution:

$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$

$$2.50 \times 40.0 = C_2 \times 500.0$$

$$100 = 500.0 \times C_2$$

$$C_2 = \frac{100}{500.0} = \mathbf{0.200 \text{ M}}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly substitutes variables into the dilution formula ($2.50 \times 40.0 = C_2 \times 500.0$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly isolates $C_2$ and calculates the final concentration as $0.200 \text{ M}$ (or $mol \ L^{-1}$).

Question 2: Water Added Calculation (Medium)

[3 Marks]

A researcher has $15.0 \text{ mL}$ of a $6.00 \text{ M}$ Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$) solution. They wish to create a $0.400 \text{ M}$ solution. Calculate the volume of distilled water (in mL) they must add to the original $15.0 \text{ mL}$ sample.

Hint: First, use $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ to find the Total Final Volume ($V_2$). Then, remember the trap: to find the water added, you must subtract the initial volume ($V_1$) from the final volume ($V_2$).

Worked Solution:

Find Total Volume ($V_2$):

$$6.00 \times 15.0 = 0.400 \times V_2$$

$$90.0 = 0.400 \times V_2$$

$$V_2 = \frac{90.0}{0.400} = 225 \text{ mL}$$

Find Water Added:

$$V_{\text{added}} = V_2 - V_1$$

$$V_{\text{added}} = 225 - 15.0 = \mathbf{210 \text{ mL}}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly sets up the initial equation to solve for $V_2$.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates the total final volume ($V_2 = 225 \text{ mL}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correctly subtracts the initial volume to find the volume of water added ($210 \text{ mL}$).

Question 3: Synthesis to Dilution Integration (Hard)

[4 Marks]

A chemist dissolves $4.20 \text{ g}$ of Sodium Bicarbonate ($NaHCO_3$) into a small beaker and transfers it to a $250 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask, filling it to the calibration mark with water. An $20.0 \text{ mL}$ aliquot of this solution is extracted and placed into a secondary $500 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask, which is then filled to the mark.

Calculate the molarity of the final solution in the $500 \text{ mL}$ flask. (Molar Mass of $NaHCO_3 = 84.01 \text{ g/mol}$)

Hint: Step 1: Calculate moles of $NaHCO_3$ using $n = m/MM$. Step 2: Find the concentration of the first flask ($C_1$) using $c = n/V$ (ensure volume is in Litres). Step 3: Use $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ using the $20.0 \text{ mL}$ aliquot as $V_1$ and $500 \text{ mL}$ as $V_2$.

Worked Solution:

Part 1: Concentration of Stock Flask

$$n = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{4.20}{84.01} = 0.0500 \text{ mol}$$

$$C_1 = \frac{n}{V} = \frac{0.0500}{0.250 \text{ L}} = 0.200 \text{ M}$$

Part 2: Dilution Calculation

The aliquot taken is $20.0 \text{ mL}$ ($V_1$), from the $0.200 \text{ M}$ stock ($C_1$). The final volume is $500 \text{ mL}$ ($V_2$).

$$C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$$

$$0.200 \times 20.0 = C_2 \times 500$$

$$4.00 = 500 \times C_2$$

$$C_2 = \frac{4.00}{500} = \mathbf{0.00800 \text{ M}}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates the moles of $NaHCO_3$ ($0.0500 \text{ mol}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates the concentration of the primary $250 \text{ mL}$ flask ($0.200 \text{ M}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correctly identifies $V_1 = 20.0 \text{ mL}$ and sets up the dilution formula.
  • 4 Marks: Correct final concentration of the secondary flask ($0.00800 \text{ M}$).

Question 4: Serial Dilution & Ion Concentration (Extension)

[5 Marks]

A stock solution of Aluminium Sulfate, $Al_2(SO_4)_3$, has a concentration of $2.50 \text{ M}$. A technician performs a serial dilution by taking a $10.0 \text{ mL}$ aliquot of the stock and diluting it to $100.0 \text{ mL}$ in Flask A. Then, a $25.0 \text{ mL}$ aliquot is taken from Flask A and diluted to $250.0 \text{ mL}$ in Flask B.

Calculate the final concentration of Sulfate ions ($[SO_4^{2-}]$) present in Flask B.

Hint: Work systematically. Calculate the concentration of the compound in Flask A. Then use that concentration as $C_1$ to calculate the concentration of the compound in Flask B. Finally, write the dissociation equation for Aluminium Sulfate to find the ratio of compound to Sulfate ions.

Worked Solution:

Part 1: First Dilution (Flask A)

$$C_{A} \times 100.0 = 2.50 \times 10.0$$

$$C_{A} = \frac{25.0}{100.0} = 0.250 \text{ M}$$

Part 2: Second Dilution (Flask B)

The aliquot taken is $25.0 \text{ mL}$ from Flask A.

$$C_{B} \times 250.0 = 0.250 \times 25.0$$

$$C_{B} = \frac{6.25}{250.0} = 0.0250 \text{ M}$$ (This is the concentration of the $Al_2(SO_4)_3$ compound).

Part 3: Ion Concentration

Dissociation equation: $Al_2(SO_4)_3 \rightarrow 2Al^{3+} + 3SO_4^{2-}$

Because there are 3 sulfate ions per formula unit, the concentration of the ion is triple the compound concentration.

$$[SO_4^{2-}] = 0.0250 \times 3 = \mathbf{0.0750 \text{ M}}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly sets up the first dilution calculation.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly determines the concentration of Flask A ($0.250 \text{ M}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correctly determines the concentration of the entire compound in Flask B ($0.0250 \text{ M}$).
  • 4 Marks: Recognises the $1:3$ stoichiometric ratio between the compound and the sulfate ions.
  • 5 Marks: Correct final ion concentration ($0.0750 \text{ M}$).
