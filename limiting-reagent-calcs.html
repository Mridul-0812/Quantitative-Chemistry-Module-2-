13. Limiting Reagent Calculations

The Formal Mathematical Approach to "Who Runs Out First?"

The Protocol: Formal Calculation Steps

In the last lesson, we looked at the *logic* of the Limiting Reagent (LR). Today, we combine that logic with the full Mass $\rightarrow$ Mole $\rightarrow$ Mole $\rightarrow$ Mass workflow. This is the "Final Boss" of stoichiometry for many students, but if you follow these five steps, you literally cannot fail.

STEP 1 Balance and Identify: Write the balanced chemical equation. If the products aren't given, use your Module 2 knowledge to deduce them. Ensure states like $(s)$, $(l)$, $(g)$, or $(aq)$ are included.

STEP 2 Find Starting Moles: Convert the given mass (grams) of both (or all) reactants into moles using: $$n = \frac{m}{MM}$$ STEP 3 The Comparison (Test Value): Divide each reactant's moles by its stoichiometric coefficient. $$\text{Test Value} = \frac{\text{Actual Moles (n)}}{\text{Coefficient in Equation}}$$ The reactant with the SMALLEST Test Value is the Limiting Reagent.

STEP 4 The Product Link: Use the actual moles of your identified Limiting Reagent (not the test value!) to calculate the moles of the product you want. $$n(\text{want}) = n(\text{have}) \times \frac{\text{Coefficient (want)}}{\text{Coefficient (have)}}$$ STEP 5 Final Conversion: Convert the moles of product back into mass (grams) or volume if required. $$m = n \times MM$$

Short Summary & Concept Recap

Why do we need a "Test Value"? Because chemicals aren't used up in a 1-to-1 race. If an equation says $2A + 1B \rightarrow C$, you are using up substance $A$ twice as fast as $B$. Even if you start with the same amount of both, $A$ will vanish first.

DO NOT BE TRICKED!

The "Test Value" found in Step 3 is a temporary ghost number. Its only purpose is to help you pick the winner. Once you have identified the Limiting Reagent, delete the test values from your brain. For all subsequent calculations (Steps 4 and 5), you must go back and use the original actual moles you calculated in Step 2.

Worked Examples

LEVEL: EASY

Example 1: The Haber Process

Nitrogen gas reacts with hydrogen gas to form ammonia ($NH_3$). If $4.0\text{ mol}$ of $N_2$ reacts with $9.0\text{ mol}$ of $H_2$, find the moles of ammonia produced.

Step 1: Balanced Equation
$N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)$

Step 2: Moles (Given)
$n(N_2) = 4.0\text{ mol}$ | $n(H_2) = 9.0\text{ mol}$

Step 3: Limiting Reagent Test
Test $N_2$: $4.0 / 1 = 4.0$
Test $H_2$: $9.0 / 3 = 3.0$
$\rightarrow 3.0 < 4.0$, so $H_2$ is the Limiting Reagent.

Step 4: Moles of Product
Use $H_2$ moles ($9.0\text{ mol}$). Ratio $NH_3 : H_2$ is $2 : 3$.
$n(NH_3) = 9.0 \times \frac{2}{3} = \mathbf{6.0\text{ mol}}$

LEVEL: MEDIUM

Example 2: Metal and Acid

A $12.15\text{g}$ strip of Magnesium is dropped into a solution containing $21.90\text{g}$ of Hydrochloric acid ($HCl$). Calculate the mass of Hydrogen gas produced.

Step 1: Equation
$Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow MgCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)$

Step 2: Starting Moles
$n(Mg) = 12.15 / 24.31 = 0.50\text{ mol}$
$n(HCl) = 21.90 / 36.46 = 0.60\text{ mol}$

Step 3: LR Test
Test $Mg$: $0.50 / 1 = 0.50$
Test $HCl$: $0.60 / 2 = 0.30$
$\rightarrow HCl$ is the Limiting Reagent.

Step 4: Product Moles
Ratio $H_2 : HCl$ is $1 : 2$.
$n(H_2) = 0.60 \times \frac{1}{2} = 0.30\text{ mol}$

Step 5: Final Mass
$m(H_2) = 0.30 \times 2.016 = \mathbf{0.605\text{g}}$

LEVEL: HARD

Example 3: Burning Propane

$22.0\text{g}$ of Propane ($C_3H_8$) is burned in $96.0\text{g}$ of Oxygen gas. Calculate the mass of Carbon Dioxide formed.

Step 1: Equation
$C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \rightarrow 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O(l)$

Step 2: Moles
$n(C_3H_8) = 22.0 / 44.1 = 0.50\text{ mol}$
$n(O_2) = 96.0 / 32.0 = 3.00\text{ mol}$

Step 3: LR Test
Test Propane: $0.50 / 1 = 0.50$
Test Oxygen: $3.00 / 5 = 0.60$
$\rightarrow$ Propane is the Limiting Reagent.

Step 4 & 5: Product
$n(CO_2) = 0.50 \times \frac{3}{1} = 1.50\text{ mol}$
$m(CO_2) = 1.50 \times 44.01 = \mathbf{66.02\text{g}}$

LEVEL: EXTENSION

Example 4: Acid + Carbonate

$10.0\text{g}$ of Calcium Carbonate is added to $0.15\text{ mol}$ of Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$). Calculate the volume of $CO_2$ gas produced at STP ($22.71\text{ L/mol}$).

Step 1: Equation (3 Products!)
$CaCO_3(s) + 2HNO_3(aq) \rightarrow Ca(NO_3)_2(aq) + CO_2(g) + H_2O(l)$

Step 2: Moles
$n(CaCO_3) = 10.0 / 100.09 = 0.10\text{ mol}$
$n(HNO_3) = 0.15\text{ mol}$ (Already in moles!)

Step 3: LR Test
Test Carbonate: $0.10 / 1 = 0.10$
Test Acid: $0.15 / 2 = 0.075$
$\rightarrow HNO_3$ is the Limiting Reagent.

Step 4: $CO_2$ Moles
Ratio $CO_2 : HNO_3$ is $1 : 2$.
$n(CO_2) = 0.15 \times 0.5 = 0.075\text{ mol}$

Step 5: Volume
$V = 0.075 \times 22.71 = \mathbf{1.70\text{ L}}$

Test Your Knowledge

Deduce the products, balance the equation, find the LR, and calculate the final quantity. (Atomic masses: $H=1.008, C=12.01, O=16.00, Al=26.98, Cl=35.45$)

Question 1 (Easy)

[2 Marks]

Methane burns in oxygen. If you have $0.8\text{ mol}$ of methane and $1.2\text{ mol}$ of oxygen, how many moles of Water are produced?

Equation: $CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O$. Test: $0.8/1$ vs $1.2/2$.

Test $CH_4 = 0.8$, Test $O_2 = 0.6$. $O_2$ is limiting.
$n(H_2O) = 1.2\text{ mol} \times (2/2) = \mathbf{1.2\text{ mol}}$.

Question 2 (Medium)

[4 Marks]

Solid Aluminium reacts with Chlorine gas to form Aluminium Chloride. If $5.4\text{g}$ of Aluminium and $21.3\text{g}$ of Chlorine are reacted, calculate the mass of product formed.

$2Al + 3Cl_2 \rightarrow 2AlCl_3$. Find moles of both first.

$n(Al) = 5.4/26.98 = 0.2\text{ mol}$. Test: $0.2/2 = 0.1$.
$n(Cl_2) = 21.3/70.9 = 0.3\text{ mol}$. Test: $0.3/3 = 0.1$.
It's a perfect tie! Both are limiting.
$n(AlCl_3) = 0.2\text{ mol}$.
$m = 0.2 \times 133.33 = \mathbf{26.67\text{g}}$.

Question 3 (Hard)

[4 Marks]

$10.0\text{g}$ of Iron (III) Oxide reacts with $5.0\text{g}$ of Carbon Monoxide gas to form Pure Iron and Carbon Dioxide. Calculate the mass of Iron produced.

$Fe_2O_3 + 3CO \rightarrow 2Fe + 3CO_2$.

$n(Fe_2O_3) = 10 / 159.7 = 0.0626\text{ mol}$ (Test: $0.0626$).
$n(CO) = 5 / 28.01 = 0.1785\text{ mol}$ (Test: $0.0595$).
$CO$ is limiting.
$n(Fe) = 0.1785 \times (2/3) = 0.119\text{ mol}$.
$m(Fe) = 0.119 \times 55.85 = \mathbf{6.65\text{g}}$.

Question 4 (Extension)

[5 Marks]

Sodium Carbonate powder ($5.3\text{g}$) is added to a beaker containing $150\text{mL}$ of $1.0\text{M}$ Hydrochloric Acid ($0.15\text{ mol}$ of $HCl$). Calculate the total mass of all aqueous products formed when the reaction stops.

Products are $NaCl(aq)$, $H_2O(l)$, and $CO_2(g)$. Only $NaCl$ is an "aqueous product".

$Na_2CO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow 2NaCl + H_2O + CO_2$.
$n(Na_2CO_3) = 5.3 / 105.99 = 0.05\text{ mol}$ (Test: $0.05$).
$n(HCl) = 0.15\text{ mol}$ (Test: $0.075$).
$Na_2CO_3$ is limiting!
$n(NaCl) = 0.05 \times 2 = 0.10\text{ mol}$.
$m(NaCl) = 0.10 \times 58.44 = \mathbf{5.84\text{g}}$.
