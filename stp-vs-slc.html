27. STP vs SLC

The Molar Volume Shortcuts ($22.71 \text{ L/mol}$ and $24.79 \text{ L/mol}$)

The Need for Standardised Conditions

In Lesson 26, we learned that the Ideal Gas Law ($PV = nRT$) can calculate any variable of a gas system. However, typing out the full $PV = nRT$ formula every time you need to convert between moles and volume is tedious, especially in stoichiometry problems where gas volume is just one step in a larger calculation.

To speed up calculations, chemists define specific, standard laboratory environments. Because Avogadro's Law tells us that equal volumes of any ideal gas contain the same number of moles, we can calculate the exact volume that precisely $1 \text{ mole}$ of any gas will occupy under these strict conditions. We call this the Molar Volume ($V_m$).

The Two Standard Conditions

  • STP (Standard Temperature and Pressure): Represents freezing conditions. Defined precisely as $0 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ ($273.15 \text{ K}$) and $100 \text{ kPa}$.
  • SLC (Standard Laboratory Conditions): Represents a typical room temperature lab. Defined precisely as $25 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ ($298.15 \text{ K}$) and $100 \text{ kPa}$.

The Shortcut Formula

If (and only if) a gas is held precisely at STP or SLC, you can bypass $PV = nRT$ entirely and use the simple Molar Volume shortcut:

$$ n = \frac{V}{V_m} $$

Where $V$ is the volume of the gas in Litres, and $V_m$ is the Molar Volume constant for that specific condition.

Worked Solutions

Review the application of the $n = \frac{V}{V_m}$ shortcut. Notice how much faster these calculations are compared to using the full $PV = nRT$ formula. However, always verify that the conditions match STP or SLC before using the shortcut!

Easy

Example 1: Volume to Moles

Calculate the number of moles of Carbon Dioxide gas ($CO_2$) present in a $50.0 \text{ L}$ container at Standard Laboratory Conditions (SLC).

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Identify the Conditions
The question explicitly states SLC. Therefore, we can use the shortcut where $V_m = 24.79 \text{ L/mol}$.

Step 2: Apply the Formula
$$ n = \frac{V}{V_m} $$ $$ n = \frac{50.0}{24.79} $$ $$ n = 2.0169... \text{ mol} $$

Final Answer: There are $\mathbf{2.02 \text{ moles}}$ of $CO_2$ (to 3 sig figs).

Medium

Example 2: Mass to Volume

Calculate the volume in Litres that $15.5 \text{ g}$ of pure Oxygen gas ($O_2$) would occupy at Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP).

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Convert Mass to Moles
$MM(O_2) = 2 \times 16.00 = 32.00 \text{ g/mol}$
$n = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{15.5}{32.00} = 0.484375 \text{ mol}$

Step 2: Identify Conditions and Apply Shortcut
The conditions are STP, so $V_m = 22.71 \text{ L/mol}$.
Rearrange $n = \frac{V}{V_m}$ to solve for Volume ($V$):
$$ V = n \times V_m $$ $$ V = 0.484375 \times 22.71 $$ $$ V = 11.0001... \text{ L} $$

Final Answer: The Oxygen gas occupies $\mathbf{11.0 \text{ L}}$ (to 3 sig figs).

Hard

Example 3: The "Non-Standard" Trap

A chemist wishes to find the volume occupied by $0.850 \text{ mol}$ of Helium gas. The laboratory is held at a comfortable $25 \ ^\circ\text{C}$, but a storm system has caused the atmospheric pressure to drop significantly to $96.5 \text{ kPa}$. Calculate the volume of the gas.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Identify the Trap!
Although the temperature is $25 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ (which matches SLC), the pressure is $96.5 \text{ kPa}$ (which does NOT match the $100 \text{ kPa}$ requirement for SLC). Because the conditions are not perfectly standard, you cannot use the $24.79 \text{ L/mol}$ shortcut! You must fall back to the master equation: $PV = nRT$.

Step 2: Setup Ideal Gas Law
$P = 96.5 \text{ kPa}$
$n = 0.850 \text{ mol}$
$R = 8.314$
$T = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 \text{ K}$

Step 3: Solve for Volume
$$ V = \frac{nRT}{P} $$ $$ V = \frac{0.850 \times 8.314 \times 298.15}{96.5} $$ $$ V = \frac{2106.99...}{96.5} = 21.834... \text{ L} $$

Final Answer: The volume is $\mathbf{21.8 \text{ L}}$. (Note: If you had incorrectly used the shortcut $V = n \times 24.79$, you would have gotten $21.1 \text{ L}$, losing marks!)

Extension

Example 4: Gas Stoichiometry at SLC

Propane gas ($C_3H_8$) undergoes complete combustion in the presence of Oxygen gas according to the equation:
$C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \rightarrow 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O(l)$

Calculate the volume of Carbon Dioxide gas produced at SLC when $100 \text{ g}$ of Propane is completely combusted.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: Calculate moles of reactant (Propane)
$MM(C_3H_8) = (3 \times 12.01) + (8 \times 1.008) = 44.094 \text{ g/mol}$
$n(C_3H_8) = \frac{m}{MM} = \frac{100}{44.094} = 2.2678... \text{ mol}$

Step 2: Molar Ratio (Stoichiometry)
Ratio of $C_3H_8 : CO_2$ is $1 : 3$.
$$ n(CO_2) = n(C_3H_8) \times 3 $$ $$ n(CO_2) = 2.2678... \times 3 = 6.8036... \text{ mol} $$

Step 3: Convert moles of product to Volume at SLC
Because the question specifies SLC, we can use the $V_m = 24.79 \text{ L/mol}$ shortcut directly on the product.
$$ V = n \times V_m $$ $$ V = 6.8036... \times 24.79 $$ $$ V = 168.66... \text{ L} $$

Final Answer: The combustion produces $\mathbf{169 \text{ L}}$ of $CO_2$ gas (to 3 sig figs).

Practise Questions

Apply your understanding of STP and SLC. Remember to check whether the conditions allow for the shortcut or if you must fall back to the Ideal Gas Law. Write your working out on paper, reveal the solutions, and self-assess using the criteria.

Question 1: Shortcut Moles (Easy)

[2 Marks]

Calculate the number of moles in a $125 \text{ L}$ sample of Hydrogen gas held strictly at Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP).

Hint: Since it is STP, use $n = V / V_m$ where $V_m = 22.71 \text{ L/mol}$.

Worked Solution:

$$ n = \frac{V}{V_m} $$

$$ n = \frac{125}{22.71} $$

$$ n = \mathbf{5.504... \text{ mol}} $$

Rounding to 3 sig figs: $\mathbf{5.50 \text{ mol}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly selects the STP molar volume ($22.71 \text{ L/mol}$) and sets up the division.
  • 2 Marks: Correct final mole calculation ($\approx 5.50 \text{ mol}$).

Question 2: Shortcut Mass (Medium)

[3 Marks]

A balloon is inflated with $8.50 \text{ L}$ of Helium gas at Standard Laboratory Conditions (SLC). Calculate the mass of the Helium gas inside the balloon.

Hint: Step 1: Use the SLC shortcut ($V_m = 24.79$) to find the moles of Helium. Step 2: Convert moles to mass using the Molar Mass of Helium from the periodic table ($m = n \times MM$).

Worked Solution:

1. Calculate Moles:
$$ n = \frac{V}{24.79} = \frac{8.50}{24.79} = 0.34288... \text{ mol} $$

2. Calculate Mass:
$MM(He) = 4.003 \text{ g/mol}$
$$ m = n \times MM $$ $$ m = 0.34288... \times 4.003 = \mathbf{1.3725... \text{ g}} $$ Rounding to 3 sig figs: $\mathbf{1.37 \text{ g}}$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly uses the $24.79$ SLC constant to find moles.
  • 2 Mark: Correctly identifies the Molar Mass of Helium ($4.003$).
  • 3 Marks: Correct final mass calculation ($\approx 1.37 \text{ g}$).

Question 3: Density Comparison (Hard)

[4 Marks]

Calculate the density of Chlorine gas ($Cl_2$) in $\text{g/L}$ at STP, and compare it to its density at SLC. State at which condition the gas is denser, and briefly explain why using KMT.

Hint: You don't need $PV=nRT$ for this! Assume you have exactly 1 mole of gas. What is the mass of 1 mole of $Cl_2$? What is the volume of 1 mole at STP ($22.71$)? What is the volume of 1 mole at SLC ($24.79$)? Use $d = m/V$ for both.

Worked Solution:

1. Determine Mass of 1 Mole:
$MM(Cl_2) = 2 \times 35.45 = 70.90 \text{ g/mol}$
Let's assume exactly $1 \text{ mole}$ of gas. The mass is $70.90 \text{ g}$.

2. Density at STP:
At STP, $1 \text{ mole}$ occupies $22.71 \text{ L}$.
$$ d(STP) = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{70.90 \text{ g}}{22.71 \text{ L}} = \mathbf{3.12 \text{ g/L}} $$

3. Density at SLC:
At SLC, $1 \text{ mole}$ occupies $24.79 \text{ L}$.
$$ d(SLC) = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{70.90 \text{ g}}{24.79 \text{ L}} = \mathbf{2.86 \text{ g/L}} $$

4. Comparison & KMT:
Chlorine is denser at STP. According to Charles' Law (KMT), the higher temperature at SLC ($25 ^\circ\text{C}$ compared to $0 ^\circ\text{C}$) gives the particles more kinetic energy, causing the gas to expand and occupy a larger volume ($24.79 \text{ L}$). Because the same mass is spread over a larger volume, the overall density decreases.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates the molar mass of $Cl_2$ ($70.90 \text{ g/mol}$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates density at STP ($3.12 \text{ g/L}$).
  • 3 Marks: Correctly calculates density at SLC ($2.86 \text{ g/L}$).
  • 4 Marks: Connects the lower density at SLC to thermal expansion (increased volume) due to higher kinetic energy.
Extension

Question 4: Synthesis (Extension)

[5 Marks]

Hydrogen Peroxide ($H_2O_2$) decomposes slowly into liquid water and Oxygen gas according to the equation:
$2H_2O_2(aq) \rightarrow 2H_2O(l) + O_2(g)$

A student completely decomposes $50.0 \text{ mL}$ of a $2.50 \text{ M}$ Hydrogen Peroxide solution in a laboratory where the temperature is $25.0 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ and the atmospheric pressure is precisely $100 \text{ kPa}$. Calculate the volume of Oxygen gas collected in Litres.

Hint:
1. Find moles of $H_2O_2$ using the concentration formula $n = c \times V$.
2. Use the stoichiometric ratio to find moles of $O_2$ produced.
3. Check the conditions! The lab is at $25 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $100 \text{ kPa}$. What standard is this? Use the shortcut!

Worked Solution:

1. Find Moles of Reactant ($H_2O_2$):
$V = 50.0 \text{ mL} = 0.0500 \text{ L}$
$n(H_2O_2) = c \times V = 2.50 \times 0.0500 = 0.125 \text{ mol}$

2. Molar Ratio (Stoichiometry):
Ratio of $H_2O_2 : O_2$ is $2 : 1$.
$$ n(O_2) = \frac{n(H_2O_2)}{2} = \frac{0.125}{2} = 0.0625 \text{ mol} $$

3. Calculate Gas Volume:
The conditions are exactly $25 \ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $100 \text{ kPa}$. This is perfectly SLC. We can use the $24.79$ shortcut.
$$ V = n \times V_m $$ $$ V = 0.0625 \times 24.79 $$ $$ V = 1.549375 \text{ L} $$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{1.55 \text{ L}}$ of Oxygen gas is produced (to 3 sig figs).

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly converts $50.0 \text{ mL}$ to Litres ($0.0500 \text{ L}$).
  • 2 Marks: Uses $c=n/V$ to find moles of $H_2O_2$ ($0.125 \text{ mol}$).
  • 3 Marks: Applies the $2:1$ ratio to find moles of $O_2$ ($0.0625 \text{ mol}$).
  • 4 Marks: Recognises the conditions match SLC and applies the $24.79$ molar volume multiplier.
  • 5 Marks: Correct final volume calculated ($\approx 1.55 \text{ L}$).
