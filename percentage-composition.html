7. Percentage Composition

Slicing the Chemical Pie by Mass

What is Percentage Composition?

Imagine you have a bag of mixed lollies. You could count them to find the percentage of jelly beans vs. snakes. But in chemistry, atoms have vastly different weights. A single Uranium atom weighs more than 200 Hydrogen atoms combined! Therefore, chemists don't care about the percentage by number of atoms; we care about the percentage by mass.

Percentage composition tells us the percentage of a compound's total mass that is contributed by each specific element. [Image of percentage composition pie chart for water]

$$ \% \text{ Element} = \frac{\text{Mass of Element in 1 mole}}{\text{Molar Mass of Compound}} \times 100 $$

Always calculate values to the number of significant figures provided by your periodic table (usually 4 s.f.).

Why do we care about this?

Percentage composition isn't just a mathematical exercise; it drives massive global industries.

Mining & Metallurgy

If a mining company finds Bauxite ore ($Al_2O_3$), they need to know exactly what percentage of that rock is actually usable Aluminium before they spend millions extracting it.

Agriculture

Fertilisers are rated by their N-P-K value. Farmers calculate the percentage composition of Nitrogen in compounds like Ammonium Nitrate ($NH_4NO_3$) to ensure optimal crop growth.

Analytical Chemistry

When forensic scientists or pharmaceutical chemists synthesize a new drug, they burn a small sample to find its percentage composition. This proves they made the correct molecule.

Worked Examples

Example 1: The Basics (Water)

Calculate the percentage composition by mass of Hydrogen and Oxygen in Water ($H_2O$).

Step 1: Calculate the Molar Mass ($MM$) of the compound.

$MM(H_2O) = (2 \times 1.008) + 16.00 = 18.016 \text{ g/mol}$

Step 2: Apply the formula for Hydrogen.

$$ \%H = \frac{\text{Mass of H}}{\text{Total Mass}} \times 100 $$

$$ \%H = \frac{(2 \times 1.008)}{18.016} \times 100 = 11.19\% $$

Step 3: Apply the formula for Oxygen.

$$ \%O = \frac{16.00}{18.016} \times 100 = 88.81\% $$

Check: $11.19\% + 88.81\% = 100.00\%$. Perfect.

Example 2: Hydrated Ionic Compounds (Hard)

Calculate the percentage of water by mass in hydrated Copper(II) Sulfate ($CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$).

Step 1: Understand the dot notation.

The "$\cdot 5H_2O$" means 5 distinct water molecules are trapped inside the crystal lattice for every 1 Copper Sulfate unit. We treat the 5 waters as one unified chunk for this calculation.

Step 2: Calculate Molar Masses.

$MM(CuSO_4) = 63.55 + 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) = 159.62 \text{ g/mol}$

$MM(5H_2O) = 5 \times 18.016 = 90.08 \text{ g/mol}$

Total Compound $MM = 159.62 + 90.08 = 249.70 \text{ g/mol}$

Step 3: Calculate Percentage of Water.

$$ \%H_2O = \frac{90.08}{249.70} \times 100 = 36.08\% $$

Practice Questions

Have your periodic table ready. Keep an eye on significant figures and always double-check that your percentages add up to approximately 100%.

Question 1

[2 Marks]

Calculate the percentage composition by mass of Carbon in Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$).

Hint: Find the molar mass of $CO_2$ first. Then divide the mass of the single Carbon atom by the total mass and multiply by 100.

Worked Solution:

$MM(CO_2) = 12.01 + (2 \times 16.00) = 44.01 \text{ g/mol}$

$$ \%C = \frac{12.01}{44.01} \times 100 $$

$$ \%C = 27.29\% $$

  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates total molar mass as $44.01 \text{ g/mol}$.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly calculates $27.29\%$.

Question 2

[3 Marks]

A farmer is deciding between two nitrogen-based fertilisers: Urea ($CH_4N_2O$) and Ammonium Sulfate ($(NH_4)_2SO_4$). Calculate the percentage composition of Nitrogen in both to determine which provides more Nitrogen per gram.

Hint: Be very careful with the brackets in Ammonium Sulfate! There are 2 Nitrogen atoms in that formula.

Worked Solution:

For Urea ($CH_4N_2O$):

$MM = 12.01 + (4 \times 1.008) + (2 \times 14.01) + 16.00 = 60.062 \text{ g/mol}$

$$ \%N = \frac{(2 \times 14.01)}{60.062} \times 100 = 46.65\% $$

For Ammonium Sulfate ($(NH_4)_2SO_4$):

$MM = (2 \times 14.01) + (8 \times 1.008) + 32.07 + (4 \times 16.00) = 132.154 \text{ g/mol}$

$$ \%N = \frac{(2 \times 14.01)}{132.154} \times 100 = 21.20\% $$

Conclusion: Urea provides more Nitrogen per gram.

  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates %N in Urea ($46.65\%$).
  • 2 Marks: Correctly accounts for 2 Nitrogens and calculates %N in Ammonium Sulfate ($21.20\%$).
  • 3 Marks: Clearly states that Urea is the better source.

Question 3

[2 Marks]

A $50.0 \text{ g}$ sample of pure Iron(III) Oxide ($Fe_2O_3$) is smelted in a blast furnace to extract pure iron. Assuming 100% efficiency, what mass of pure iron can be extracted?

Hint: This is a two-step problem. First, find the percentage of Iron in $Fe_2O_3$. Second, find that percentage of your $50.0 \text{ g}$ sample.

Worked Solution:

Step 1: % of Fe in $Fe_2O_3$

$MM(Fe_2O_3) = (2 \times 55.85) + (3 \times 16.00) = 159.70 \text{ g/mol}$

$$ \%Fe = \frac{(2 \times 55.85)}{159.70} \times 100 = 69.94\% $$

Step 2: Find Mass in Sample

Mass of Fe $= 69.94\% \text{ of } 50.0 \text{ g}$

$$ \text{Mass} = 0.6994 \times 50.0 = 34.97 \text{ g} $$

  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates the percentage composition of Iron as $69.94\%$.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly applies the percentage to the $50.0\text{g}$ mass to find $34.97\text{g}$.
