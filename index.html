Welcome to Quantitative Chemistry

Year 11 Preliminary - Module 2

The Mathematics of Chemistry

Welcome to Module 2! Quantitative Chemistry is often considered the most mathematically rigorous component of the Year 11 syllabus. However, it is fundamentally built upon a few core, logical principles. Rather than memorising disparate equations, this programme is designed to help you understand the relationships between mass, moles, volume, and concentration.

Whether you are calculating the volume of gas produced in an engine, or standardising a solution in the laboratory, the principles you learn here will carry you straight through to your HSC examinations.

How to Use This Platform

To prevent cognitive overload, this platform breaks the module down into strictly focused, bite-sized lessons. Navigation is simple:

  • Sidebar: Use the left-hand menu to jump between specific topics. They are ordered chronologically.
  • Content & Worked Solutions: Each page begins with brief theory, followed by four worked solutions (Easy, Medium, Hard, and Extension).
  • Interactive Practise: At the bottom of each page, you will find NESA-style practise questions.

The Self-Marking Engine

Honesty is the best policy! When you complete a practise question, you can access hints and ultimately reveal the NESA-style marking criteria. You will then assign yourself a mark based on that criteria. Your total marks are tracked across the entire site and will be printed on your final certificate.

Practise Question: Basic Stoichiometry (Demo)

[3 Marks]

Calculate the molar mass of Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$).

(Use the standard atomic weights: Ca = 40.08, C = 12.01, O = 16.00)

Hint: Remember that the subscript '3' only applies to the Oxygen. You need to add the mass of 1 Calcium, 1 Carbon, and 3 Oxygens.

Worked Solution:

$$MM(CaCO_3) = 40.08 + 12.01 + (3 \times 16.00)$$

$$MM(CaCO_3) = 40.08 + 12.01 + 48.00$$

$$MM(CaCO_3) = 100.09 \text{ g/mol}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly identifies the atomic masses of Ca, C, and O.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly multiplies the mass of Oxygen by 3.
  • 3 Marks: Calculates the final answer correctly with appropriate units (g/mol).
