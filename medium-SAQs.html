Question 1: The Combustion of Magnesium [8 Marks]

A student reacts 4.86 g of Magnesium ribbon with 4.00 g of Oxygen gas to produce Magnesium Oxide ($MgO$).

2 Marks

a) Write a balanced chemical equation for this reaction, including states.

3 Marks

b) Determine which reactant is the limiting reagent through calculation.

3 Marks

c) Calculate the mass of $MgO$ produced and the mass of the excess reagent remaining.

Hint: First convert both masses to moles. Use the mole ratio from your balanced equation ($2:1$) to compare how much of each you actually need versus what you have.

Marking Criteria

MarksRequirements
7-8Correct equation, clear LR identification, correct final mass for both product and excess (3 sig figs).
4-6Correct LR but minor error in excess reagent mass or molar mass values.

Detailed Worked Solution

a) Balanced Equation:
$2Mg(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2MgO(s)$

b) Finding Limiting Reagent:
$n(Mg) = \frac{4.86}{24.31} = 0.200 \text{ mol}$
$n(O_2) = \frac{4.00}{32.00} = 0.125 \text{ mol}$
Required $n(O_2)$ for all $Mg = \frac{0.200}{2} = 0.100 \text{ mol}$.
Since we have $0.125 \text{ mol}$ of $O_2$, $Mg$ is the limiting reagent.

c) Mass Calculations:
$n(MgO) = n(Mg) = 0.200 \text{ mol}$
$m(MgO) = 0.200 \times (24.31 + 16.00) = 8.06 \text{ g}$
Excess $O_2$ used $= 0.100 \text{ mol}$. Remaining $= 0.125 - 0.100 = 0.025 \text{ mol}$.
$m(O_2 \text{ excess}) = 0.025 \times 32.00 = 0.800 \text{ g}$.

An experiment was conducted to observe the volume of a gas at different temperatures at constant pressure. Plot the data and extrapolate to find Absolute Zero.

Click to plot points. (X-axis: Temp, Y-axis: Vol)

3 Marks

a) Plot the points and draw a line of best fit. Extrapolate back to $V = 0$.

4 Marks

b) Using the gradient formula ($y = mx + c$), determine the theoretical temperature where volume is zero. Explain the significance of this value.

Worked Solution

a) Graphing: The line should be a straight diagonal increasing from left to right. When extrapolated backwards (to the left), it should cross the X-axis (Temperature) at approximately $-273^\circ C$.

b) Calculation:
Find gradient $m$: $\frac{137 - 100}{100 - 0} = 0.37$
Equation: $V = 0.37T + 100$
Set $V = 0$: $0 = 0.37T + 100 \rightarrow T = \frac{-100}{0.37} \approx -270.3^\circ C$.
Significance: This represents 0 Kelvin, the temperature at which molecular motion theoretically ceases.

Question 3: Identifying an Unknown Organic Compound [6 Marks]

A compound is found to contain $85.7\%$ Carbon and $14.3\%$ Hydrogen by mass. Its molar mass is determined to be $56.12 \text{ g/mol}$.

3 Marks

a) Determine the empirical formula of the compound.

3 Marks

b) Determine the molecular formula of the compound.

Detailed Solution

Empirical Formula:
$n(C) = 85.7 / 12.01 = 7.136 \text{ mol}$
$n(H) = 14.3 / 1.008 = 14.187 \text{ mol}$
Divide by smallest ($7.136$):
$C = 1, H = 1.99 \approx 2$.
Empirical Formula: $CH_2$

Molecular Formula:
$EMM (CH_2) = 12.01 + 2(1.008) = 14.026 \text{ g/mol}$
Multiplier $n = \frac{56.12}{14.026} \approx 4$.
Molecular formula $= 4 \times (CH_2) = \mathbf{C_4H_8}$

Question 4: Standard Solution Preparation [6 Marks]

A lab technician needs to prepare $500 \text{ mL}$ of a $0.100 \text{ M}$ solution of Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$).

2 Marks

a) Calculate the mass of solid $NaOH$ pellets required.

4 Marks

b) Describe the procedure for preparing this standard solution using a volumetric flask. Include two safety precautions.

Solution

a) Calculation:
$n = C \times V = 0.100 \times 0.500 = 0.050 \text{ mol}$
$m = n \times MM = 0.050 \times (22.99 + 16.00 + 1.008) = 2.00 \text{ g}$

b) Procedure:
1. Weigh 2.00 g $NaOH$ in a beaker.
2. Dissolve in a small volume of deionised water.
3. Transfer to $500 \text{ mL}$ volumetric flask using a funnel.
4. Rinse beaker and add washings to flask.
5. Fill to mark (meniscus) and invert to mix.
Safety: $NaOH$ is corrosive; wear gloves and goggles. Heat is released when dissolving; add solid to water slowly.

Question 5: Decomposition of Sodium Azide [8 Marks]

Airbags deploy using the reaction: $2NaN_3(s) \rightarrow 2Na(s) + 3N_2(g)$.

4 Marks

a) Calculate the volume of $N_2$ gas produced from $65.0 \text{ g}$ of $NaN_3$ at Standard Laboratory Conditions (SLC: $100 \text{ kPa}, 298 \text{ K}$). $V_m = 24.79 \text{ L/mol}$.

4 Marks

b) If the reaction takes place at $80^\circ C$ and $110 \text{ kPa}$, what would be the new volume? (Use Ideal Gas Law).

Worked Solution

a) Volume at SLC:
$MM(NaN_3) = 22.99 + (3 \times 14.01) = 65.02 \text{ g/mol}$
$n(NaN_3) = 65.0 / 65.02 \approx 1.00 \text{ mol}$
Ratio $NaN_3 : N_2 = 2:3$. So $n(N_2) = 1.50 \text{ mol}$.
$V = 1.50 \times 24.79 = \mathbf{37.19 \text{ L}}$

b) Non-Standard Conditions:
$P = 110 \text{ kPa}, T = 80 + 273.15 = 353.15 \text{ K}$
$V = \frac{nRT}{P} = \frac{1.50 \times 8.314 \times 353.15}{110} = \mathbf{40.05 \text{ L}}$
